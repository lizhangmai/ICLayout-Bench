11:T6a30,<h1 id="problem-output-controlled-auxiliary-compensation-ota-layout-task">Output-Controlled Auxiliary Compensation OTA Layout Task</h1>
<h2 id="problem-objective">Objective</h2>
<p>Lay out <code>amp_006_leung_dfcfc1</code>, retaining the complete folded PMOS-input stage,
three-stage signal path, direct output feedforward, and auxiliary compensation.
The folded stage drives <code>net050</code>, which drives the output PMOS and the
PMOS/diode/mirror path to <code>net049</code>. That node drives both the output NMOS
and an auxiliary common-source NMOS whose drain is <code>net1</code>; a biased PMOS
loads this auxiliary drain. C0 connects <code>net050</code> to output, while C1 connects
<code>net049</code> to <code>net1</code>. The auxiliary branch therefore acts on the NMOS output
control node. In contrast, the separate AMP007 case's auxiliary PMOS senses
<code>net050</code> and its compensation terminates at <code>net2</code>.</p>
<h3 id="problem-optimization-objective">Optimization objective</h3>
<p>Maximize the declared quality score within the solve budget while satisfying all physical and necessary functional requirements. The first passing DRC, LVS and required electrical checks establish a feasible baseline. Continue improving the scored metrics from that baseline; a passing check or <code>task_success = true</code> does not mean that the quality objectives have been achieved.</p>
<h2 id="problem-inputs-and-interface">Inputs and Interface</h2>
<ul>
<li><code>materials/circuit.cdl</code>: authoritative native physical device graph.</li>
<li><code>materials/circuit.spice</code>: matching simulator representation with logical body nets.</li>
<li><code>materials/testbench.spice</code>: public bias, bilateral AC and step measurements.</li>
</ul>
<p>Ordered ports: <code>vss vdd vinn vinp vout net013</code>. VSS is return, VDD supply,
VINN/VINP the inverting/noninverting inputs, VOUT output, and <code>net013</code> the
external bias-sink connection. Input PMOS bodies connect to the separate
<code>net31</code> source-tied well; other PMOS use the VDD well and NMOS use the VSS
substrate. Preserve all 26 MOS groups, W/L/m,
body connections, and both internal capacitive branches. Native equivalent
parallel fingering is allowed only when all physical and electrical checks pass.</p>
<p>MOS dimensions retain the declared grid adaptation. All capacitors are physical <code>cap_cmim</code>:</p>
<ul>
<li><code>c0</code>: <code>net050</code> (top plate) to <code>vout</code> (bottom plate), 8 parallel 48.935 × 48.935 um MIM units.</li>
<li><code>c1</code>: <code>net049</code> (top plate) to <code>net1</code> (bottom plate), 3 parallel 50.94 × 50.94 um MIM units.</li>
</ul>
<p>These are internal compensation devices, distinct from the external load.
The normalized source's 27.4754 uA bias is deliberately calibrated to
6.86885 uA, improving headroom and finite tracking at the declared signal
levels. No ideal output servo or internal numerical damping element is added.</p>
<h2 id="problem-operating-conditions">Operating Conditions</h2>
<p>Use typical IHP LV MOS/MIM/poly models at 27 C and VDD=1.2 V, with an external
6.86885 uA sink from <code>net013</code> to VSS. Every combination of 5/10/20 pF output
load and 0.5/0.7 V DC input must pass. A zero-volt source from VINN (<code>vm</code>) to
VOUT closes unity feedback. VINP is AC ground. The two DC input levels
independently check operating-point error and the complete sampled return ratio.</p>
<p>At the closed input/output boundary, inject a series AC voltage with zero
parallel AC current, then zero series voltage and unit current into <code>vm</code>.
Both injections retain the original circuit and loading. With first-run
<code>b=-I(VPROBE)</code>, <code>d=V(vm)</code> and second-run <code>a=-I(VPROBE)</code>, <code>c=V(vm)</code>, define
<code>delta=a*d-b*c</code> and the bilateral return ratio
<code>T=(2*delta-a+d)/(1-2*delta+a-d)</code> (Tian et al., Eq. 30).
The deck exports both injections' voltages/currents for independent two-port
admittance reconstruction. This is a finite external-loop measurement,
not a certificate for every internal pole or a complete Nyquist proof.</p>
<p>Sweep 0.01 Hz–10 GHz at 300 points/decade. Use continuous phase starting
on its natural low-frequency branch, without a phase-offset correction.
Check both first and final descending unity crossings, minimum sampled
<code>abs(1+T)</code>, maximum absolute continuous phase of <code>1+T</code> across the entire sweep,
and maximum gain over 2–10 GHz. Missing crossings or invalid measurements
are evaluator errors; both first and final phase margins must lie in [0, 180] degrees.
Minimum return distance and return-phase excursion have only nonnegative
domain bounds; high-frequency gain has no acceptance bound. These full-sweep
observations affect continuous quality and do not establish a stability certificate.</p>
<p>For each condition, VINP's pulse starts at 0.5 V, rises to 0.7 V at
2–2.02 us and falls at 10.02–10.04 us, with a 16 us period. Transient uses
its own pulse-start operating point, independently of the AC DC-input setting.
Simulate 0–18 us with the KLU linear solver and Gear order 2, 2 ns maximum step, <code>itl4=1000</code>,
<code>rshunt=1e12</code>, <code>reltol=1e-5</code>, <code>abstol=1e-12</code>, and <code>vntol=1e-8</code>.
The numerical shunt is not a physical device. The maintained 1 pA absolute
current tolerance is checked at 0.1 pA with tenfold tighter relative/voltage
tolerances and a 1e13 ohm shunt. Initial experimental 10 fA/1 fA current
tolerances produced excessive iteration cost in the extracted low-resistance
network. This numerical-floor calibration preserves every electrical bound
and all physical/parasitic elements; half-step and trapezoidal controls
independently check the observable dynamics. PVT, mismatch, noise,
distortion, supply startup, rail-to-rail operation and arbitrary-load stability
are outside this contract.</p>
<h2 id="problem-physical-requirements">Physical Requirements</h2>
<p>Submit GDSII top cell <code>amp_006_leung_dfcfc1</code>, at most 32 MiB. Pass native
IHP main/maximal DRC without waivers (standalone scope, density/antenna off),
strict named-interface LVS with physically verified body connections, and a 6400 × 130 um functional
envelope. Every device/contact/interconnect drawing layer listed in the
runtime <code>outline</code> constraint contributes to area; annotation/pin-purpose
layers do not. Labels alone do not establish electrical connectivity.</p>
<p>Post-layout simulation uses the submitted GDS's complete Magic RC, with
physical MIM devices and interconnect resistances/capacitances. Half-grid
import is explicit. Magic retains only Metal3 interface labels in its isolated
extraction copy to avoid repeated internal well-name aliases, while native LVS
checks the original GDS and its actual body-contact connectivity. No resistance/capacitance threshold omits small parasitics.
Source simulation and extraction use the same logical body-net boundary. Distributed substrate resistance/noise and fabrication
signoff remain unqualified. The long independent witness is a feasibility
layout, not an area optimum.</p>
<h2 id="problem-electrical-requirements-and-scoring">Electrical Requirements and Scoring</h2>
<p>Artifact, geometry, DRC, LVS and declared functional requirements must pass. Parasitic extraction is also mandatory: it must produce a complete, electrically consistent netlist usable by the required post-layout simulations. A completed candidate rejection scores zero; infrastructure errors do not establish candidate failure.</p>
<p>For <code>layout</code> scoring, cap every positively weighted raw quality at 1 before aggregation; the score ranges from 0 to 100. Reaching every weighted target scores 100, and no minimum score is required for validity.</p>
<p>Physical checks and the declared functional bounds must pass. Each <code>source_*</code> job and its corresponding candidate extracted circuit use exactly the same testbench, model resources, parameters, loads and measurement windows. Every electrical anchor is its paired pre-layout observation, measuring preservation of the fixed circuit's performance at that condition. Reports retain every paired result.</p>
<p>Let x be the post-layout measurement and b its same-condition pre-layout measurement:</p>
<ul>
<li>Ratio normalization: maximize with q = x/b and minimize with q = b/x. When a numerical scale s is declared, use (x+s)/(b+s) and its reciprocal, respectively. The scale handles zero values as a normalization floor; it is neither an allowed degradation nor an acceptance bound.</li>
<li>Amplitude in dB: maximize with q = 10^((x-b)/20) and minimize with its reciprocal.</li>
<li>Target normalization: q = 1/(1+abs(x-b)/s), where s is the declared physical scale. Signed or zero-valued operating points are not divided directly.</li>
</ul>
<p>Use <code>layout</code> scoring. First compute each metric's raw quality q from the worst paired condition, with raw area quality q_area = area_target / candidate_functional_area. Credit c_i = min(1, q_i) for every positively weighted metric, then compute S = 100 * product(c_i ** w_i). The weights sum to 1 and the score is bounded by 100. An improvement beyond one target cannot compensate for missing another target. Physical, extraction, or functional rejection scores zero; other missing or invalid measurements leave the score unknown, and zero-weight observations must also be valid. Reaching every positively weighted target scores 100; a feasible reference may score far below 100. Weights express quality tradeoffs, not acceptance bounds.</p>
<p>The measurements below use the supplied SPICE node and source names. <code>v(n)</code> is a node voltage; <code>i(V)</code> is current flowing into a voltage source, so delivered power uses a negative sign. <code>db(z)=20*log10(abs(z))</code>; <code>mag/abs</code> denote magnitude, and <code>cph</code> denotes continuous phase in radians. <code>find</code>, <code>when</code>, <code>from/to</code> and <code>rise/fall</code> follow the deck's interpolation, window and crossing conventions. SPICE suffixes p/n/u/m/meg/g mean pico/nano/micro/milli/mega/giga. Nested <code>avg</code>, <code>min</code>, <code>max</code>, <code>vecmin/vecmax</code> and <code>deriv</code> represent window averages, extrema and sampled derivatives. Measure each operating condition independently, pair it with the source result under that condition, and take the worst paired quality within each metric group.</p>
<p>For metrics declaring <code>saturating_ratio</code>, q = 2(b+s)/(b+x+2*s), using the quality anchor b and the positive scale s in the metric’s units. Equality to the anchor gives raw quality 1; improvements approach raw quality 2 but credited quality stays at 1. Normalize each condition before selecting the worst quality. These scales and targets do not add acceptance cutoffs.</p>

<h3 id="problem-quality-targets-and-weights">Quality targets and weights</h3>
<p>For a minimizing metric, additional loss is <code>max(0, x-b)</code>; for a maximizing metric it is <code>max(0, b-x)</code>. Degradation quality is <code>q = s/(s+loss)</code>: loss equal to the declared budget halves that metric's quality, while matching or improving the paired source receives full credit. Normalize each condition before taking the worst quality. This formula does not change functional requirements.</p>
<p>The area quality target is <strong>30000 um2</strong>. It is an integration budget, not a hard limit or a proven process minimum.</p>
<p>The eleven fixed MIM plates occupy 26941.7246 um2, or 89.8% of the 30000 um2
footprint objective, leaving 3058.2754 um2 for plate spacing, enclosures and
interconnect. MOS and contact layers may share footprint with MIM only where
native rules allow. The fixed area objective is unchanged.</p>
<p>Return-distance loss retains its 0.05 budget. Around the source distance of 0.8,
this loss raises the sampled peak sensitivity <code>1/min(abs(1+T))</code> by about 7%.
It measures sampled external-loop robustness, without certifying every internal
loop. Gain and phase-margin diagnostics retain zero weight.</p>
<p><code>settling_time_s</code> measures the tail of both step responses. For the rising step,
measure from the ramp end at 2.02 us until the output enters and remains within
200 uV of <code>high_v</code> until 9.5 us; <code>high_v</code> is the mean over 9-9.5 us. For the
falling step, measure from 10.04 us using 200 uV around <code>low_v</code> until 17.5 us;
<code>low_v</code> is the mean over 17-17.5 us. The band is 0.1% of the commanded 0.2 V
step, independent of each circuit's DC offset. Interpolate the last band-entry
crossing linearly between native samples. An already settled response has zero
duration. If the endpoint is outside the band, report the full observation
duration (7.48 us up or 7.46 us down); this is a censored quality observation,
not a functional rejection. Compare each direction and condition with its own
pre-layout duration, then take the worst paired quality. The additional-loss
budget is 20 ns, one declared input-ramp interval. This finite-window measure
does not guarantee asymptotic convergence.</p>

prints <code>power_normalized = power_w / power_unit</code> and
<code>output_error_normalized = output_error_v / error_unit</code>:</p>
