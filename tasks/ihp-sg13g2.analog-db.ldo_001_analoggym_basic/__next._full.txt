11:T6835,<h1 id="problem-multistage-error-amplifier-pmos-regulator-layout-task">Multistage Error-Amplifier PMOS Regulator Layout Task</h1>
<h2 id="problem-objective">Objective</h2>
<p>Lay out <code>ldo_001_analoggym_basic</code>: a PMOS pass regulator with a folded
PMOS-input error amplifier, cascoded bias branches, mirrored loads and an
additional PMOS/NMOS intermediate branch driving the pass-gate node <code>net1</code>.
The auxiliary PMOS source and body connect to <code>net1</code>; the input pair has a
separate source-tied <code>net20</code> well. Internal compensation connects <code>net106</code>
to output and the 3:1 physical divider returns one quarter of output voltage.
This complete multistage error amplifier and its internal pass-gate drive
are distinct from the simpler 5T and mirror-OTA regulator cases.</p>
<h3 id="problem-optimization-objective">Optimization objective</h3>
<p>Maximize the declared quality score within the solve budget while satisfying all physical and necessary functional requirements. The first passing DRC, LVS and required electrical checks establish a feasible baseline. Continue improving the scored metrics from that baseline; a passing check or <code>task_success = true</code> does not mean that the quality objectives have been achieved.</p>
<h2 id="problem-inputs-and-interface">Inputs and Interface</h2>
<ul>
<li><code>materials/circuit.cdl</code>: authoritative native physical circuit.</li>
<li><code>materials/circuit.spice</code>: matching simulator circuit with logical body nets.</li>
<li><code>materials/testbench.spice</code>: bilateral loop and current-load recovery.</li>
<li><code>materials/startup.spice</code>: synchronized zero-state resistive startup.</li>
<li><code>materials/sweeps.spice</code>: line/load and downward headroom sweeps.</li>
</ul>
<p>Ordered ports: <code>vdd vout vss ib vref vfb sense</code>. VDD/VSS are supply/return,
VOUT output, IB connects an external current sink to VSS, and VREF is the
external reference. VFB is the physical divider midpoint and SENSE is the
inverting error-amplifier input. Connect them only through the testbench's
zero-volt injection link; no additional servo or feedback element is permitted.
The source's zero-volt output measurement link is collapsed into VOUT and
relocated to this explicitly declared divider/input boundary.</p>
<p>Retain all 24 source MOS groups. The declared inputs retain upstream W/L/m with the reviewed geometry-grid adaptation. Other PMOS bodies use the VDD well;
NMOS bodies and physical high-poly backplates use the VSS substrate contact.
Well/substrate contacts must meet the native PDK rules. Preserve the declared
MOS dimensions and total multiplicities; equivalent parallel
fingering requires all physical and electrical checks to pass.</p>
<p>All internal passives are physical DUT elements:</p>
<ul>
<li>Three parallel 42.685 × 42.685 um <code>cap_cmim</code> units: top <code>net106</code>, bottom VOUT;
approximately 8.22 pF total, retaining the source's approximately 8.2 pF branch.</li>
<li>One 51.64 × 51.64 um <code>cap_cmim</code>: top <code>net10</code>, bottom VOUT, approximately
4 pF. This added physical local compensation closes the internal dynamic mode.</li>
<li>Twelve series <code>rhigh</code> segments from VOUT to VFB and four from VFB to VSS,
each W=1 um, L=17.465 um, b=0: approximately 300/100 kohm total.</li>
</ul>
<p>There is no additional external output capacitor, but the compensation is
real output-connected storage. Do not delete or idealize any internal passive.</p>
<h2 id="problem-operating-conditions">Operating Conditions</h2>
<p>The loop/load testbench allows 1000 DC operating-point iterations (<code>itl1=1000</code>)
for both source and extracted simulations. Error tolerances and physical stimuli
are unchanged; this is a convergence setting, not added circuit damping.</p>
<p>Use typical IHP LV MOS/MIM/poly models at 27 C. VDD is 1.2 or 1.3 V,
VREF=0.225 V, and the external IB-to-VSS sink is 8 uA. The reference is
explicitly changed from the normalized source's 0.4 V: the 3:1 divider then
has a 0.9 V ideal output target, within the LV supply. The 8 uA bias and
all original total MOS widths/lengths are retained. This is a bounded
regulator core, not a precision reference, PVT or manufacturing-signoff task.</p>
<p>Main measurements cover both supplies and initial current loads 0.2/0.5 mA.
After an operating point, the load doubles at 2–2.2 us, remains high for
8 us, and returns at 10.2–10.4 us; the period is 16 us. Simulate 0–18 us
with KLU, Gear order 2, maximum step 1 ns, <code>rshunt=1e12</code>, <code>reltol=1e-5</code>,
<code>abstol=1e-12</code> and <code>vntol=1e-8</code>. The numerical shunt is not a physical
bias or startup device. Explicit <code>.save</code> lists retain all measured vectors;
every extracted circuit element still participates in the solve.</p>
<p>Measure the bilateral return ratio at the actual divider/input boundary:
first inject unit series AC voltage from SENSE to VFB, with zero parallel
current; then use zero series voltage and unit current into SENSE. With
first-run <code>b=-I(VPROBE)</code>, <code>d=V(SENSE)</code> and second-run <code>a=-I(VPROBE)</code>,
<code>c=V(SENSE)</code>, define <code>delta=a*d-b*c</code> and
<code>T=(2*delta-a+d)/(1-2*delta+a-d)</code> (Tian et al., Eq. 30).
Both injections retain divider, input and reverse-path loading. Exported
voltages/currents permit an independent two-port admittance calculation.
Sweep 0.01 Hz–1 GHz at 300 points/decade, using continuous natural phase
without an arbitrary low-frequency offset. Check first/final descending
unity crossings, minimum sampled <code>abs(1+T)</code>, full sampled return-difference
phase and maximum 200 MHz–1 GHz gain. This finite external-loop contract
is not an exhaustive proof of all internal poles.</p>
<p>Sweep load 0.2–1 mA in 10 uA steps at each supply. Downward VDD sweeps
1.3–0.8 V in 1 mV steps at 0.2/1 mA measure the first 30 mV output-error
crossing. The line span uses both endpoints of 1.2–1.3 V. The regulation
floor includes amplifier headroom and is not a pass-resistance-only dropout
rating. These DC sweeps do not extend dynamic stability to every intermediate
supply/load combination.</p>
<p>Startup uses <code>uic</code> with supply, reference and bias ramping together from zero
in 10 or 20 us. For both supplies, use 900/4500 ohm resistive loads (nominally
1/0.2 mA at 0.9 V), maximum step 2 ns, and simulate to 50 us. This does not
qualify arbitrary sequencing or a constant-current load imposed at zero supply.
Faster 1 us startup and 20 ns current-load edges are outside qualification: they
produce excessive peaks in the same extracted circuit. The README provides
runnable failure probes. The maintained contract explicitly narrows the
initial development stimuli without relaxing the voltage-excursion bounds.
Loads below 0.2 mA are outside the qualified dynamic range.</p>
<p>The phase margins at the first and last crossings must both lie within [0, 180] degrees. Minimum return distance and return-phase excursion need only be nonnegative; high-frequency gain has no acceptance threshold. These full-sweep metrics affect continuous quality without adding stability acceptance conditions. Missing crossings or invalid observations are evaluation errors.</p>
<h2 id="problem-physical-requirements">Physical Requirements</h2>
<p>Submit GDSII top cell <code>ldo_001_analoggym_basic</code>, at most 32 MiB. Pass native
IHP main/maximal DRC without waivers (standalone density/antenna disabled),
strict named-interface LVS with physically verified body connections, and a 5500 × 360 um functional
envelope. All device/contact/interconnect drawing layers in the runtime
<code>outline</code> contribute to area; annotation and pin-purpose layers do not.</p>
<p>Candidate-derived Magic RC includes both capacitor plates, every poly segment,
MOS and interconnect parasitic. Half-grid import and zero parasitic thresholds
are explicit. Native LVS checks the original GDS and actual body connections; the Magic
extraction copy retains only Metal3 interface text, avoiding internal well-name
aliases without altering geometry. Source simulation and extraction
use the same logical body-net boundary. Distributed substrate
resistance/noise and fabrication signoff remain unqualified.</p>
<h2 id="problem-electrical-requirements-and-scoring">Electrical Requirements and Scoring</h2>
<p>Artifact, geometry, DRC, LVS and declared functional requirements must pass. Parasitic extraction is also mandatory: it must produce a complete, electrically consistent netlist usable by the required post-layout simulations. A completed candidate rejection scores zero; infrastructure errors do not establish candidate failure.</p>
<p>Physical checks and the declared functional bounds must pass. Quality scoring imposes no fixed degradation allowance. Each <code>source_*</code> job and its corresponding candidate extracted circuit use exactly the same testbench, model resources, parameters, loads and measurement windows. Independent source observations define the 100-point electrical baseline without depending on the submitted GDS; reports retain every paired result.</p>
<p>Let x be the post-layout measurement and b the source-circuit measurement under the same conditions:</p>
<ul>
<li>Ratio normalization: maximize with q = x/b and minimize with q = b/x. When a numerical scale s is declared, use (x+s)/(b+s) and its reciprocal, respectively. The scale handles zero values as a normalization floor; it is neither an allowed degradation nor an acceptance bound.</li>
<li>Amplitude in dB: maximize with q = 10^((x-b)/20) and minimize with its reciprocal.</li>
<li>Target normalization: q = 1/(1+abs(x-b)/s), where s is the declared physical scale. Signed or zero-valued operating points are not divided directly.</li>
</ul>
<p>Use <code>layout</code> scoring. Each metric takes its worst paired quality q, and the total score is S = 100 * product(min(1, q_i) ** w_i), with area quality q_area = area_reference / candidate_functional_area. The weights below sum to 1; metric dimensions describe measurements and do not determine weights. Physical, extraction, or functional rejection scores zero; other missing or invalid measurements leave the score unknown, and zero-weight observations must also be valid. Matching source-circuit performance at the reference area scores 100 points; each metric is capped at quality 1, so scores cannot exceed 100. Weights express quality tradeoffs rather than hard bounds.</p>
<p>The measurements below use the supplied SPICE node and source names. <code>v(n)</code> is a node voltage; <code>i(V)</code> is current flowing into a voltage source, so delivered power uses a negative sign. <code>db(z)=20*log10(abs(z))</code>; <code>mag/abs</code> denote magnitude, and <code>cph</code> denotes continuous phase in radians. <code>find</code>, <code>when</code>, <code>from/to</code> and <code>rise/fall</code> follow the deck's interpolation, window and crossing conventions. SPICE suffixes p/n/u/m/meg/g mean pico/nano/micro/milli/mega/giga. Nested <code>avg</code>, <code>min</code>, <code>max</code>, <code>vecmin/vecmax</code> and <code>deriv</code> represent window averages, extrema and sampled derivatives. Measure each operating condition independently, pair it with the source result under that condition, and take the worst paired quality within each metric group.</p>
<p><code>loaded_ripple_v</code>, <code>released_ripple_v</code>, <code>startup_error_v</code>, <code>recovery_load_v</code>, <code>recovery_release_v</code>, <code>line_span_v</code>, <code>load_span_v</code> use <code>saturating_ratio</code>: q = 2(b+s)/(b+x+2s), where x is candidate error, b is paired source error and s is the positive scale below. Source equivalence gives q=1; improvement approaches the upper limit 2 with diminishing returns. s retains the declared units and numeric-floor meaning; normalize each condition before taking the worst quality, and apply other rules as declared.</p>

<h2 id="problem-tools-and-submission">Tools and Submission</h2>
<p>Soft solve budget: <strong>3 hours</strong>.</p>
<p>Discover task/resources/feedback through <code>/protocol/task.json</code> (including its <code>resources</code> field) and <code>/protocol/harness.json</code>. Use the supplied
IHP primitives and KLayout/Magic/ngspice. Write <code>/workspace/output/final.gds</code>
and explicitly submit via the harness protocol. Host configuration, source
records and reference answers are excluded from standard solver inputs.</p>
<p>The authoritative MOS input preserves the original logical groups, W/L/m and all four terminal nets. Verified unit arrays, multiple fingers, shared diffusion and shared wells on the same body net are allowed. Native LVS checks connectivity and equivalent device parameters; native DRC checks actual well/substrate contacts. Contact count, dimensions, area/perimeter and the reference well partition are not circuit constraints. PEX uses actual diffusion geometry and interconnect RC, with logical body nets as its boundary; distributed substrate/well resistance is outside this extraction model.</p>
<h3 id="problem-solve-workflow">Solve workflow</h3>
<ol>
<li>Read the declared circuit and materials, <code>/protocol/task.json</code> for paths, constraints and reviewed resources, and <code>/protocol/harness.json</code> for capabilities and helper commands. Task inputs under <code>/task</code> and resources under <code>/resources</code> are read-only; construct the layout in <code>/workspace</code> with the declared tools. With layout MCP, paths and commands refer to the remote EDA workspace. Create your own implementation with real devices, interconnect and pins, using your own tool loop without delegating to other agents; reference layouts and author code are not solver inputs.</li>
<li>Inspect <code>resources.external</code> before using external tools. Each command starts a fresh shell: when tool modules are declared, source <code>/usr/share/Modules/init/bash</code> and load the selected tool's modules; export its declared <code>tool_environment</code> settings in that same command. Repeat as needed and use batch or headless operation. Local manuals, command references, and release notes may be available within the declared EDA installation and PDK resource directories. Locate these resources through <code>/protocol/task.json</code> and consult the documentation for the installed tool version when diagnosing unfamiliar syntax or tool failures.</li>
<li>Generate an initial candidate and submit a snapshot early. When <code>process-feedback</code> is available, check the current GDS with layout MCP <code>check</code> or <code>python -I /protocol/process_check.py</code> before the final submission. This runs the final evaluator's complete plan and returns engineering diagnostics and candidate measurements, without scores or normalized quality factors. Checks have no count limit and consume the same wall-clock budget. Inspect the physical and functional results and all scored metrics; successful tool execution alone does not establish requirement satisfaction. Read paginated diagnostics with <code>report</code> when using MCP. Local exploratory checks do not replace this complete check.</li>
<li>Use the remaining time for measured improvements to the declared quality objectives. Identify the limiting scored metrics, make targeted layout changes, regenerate and check the candidate, and compare measurements with the previous candidate and the stated scoring targets and trade-offs. Preserve the best valid candidate and revert changes that worsen the overall objective. Repeat while time and useful improvements remain; passing validity checks alone is not a stopping condition.</li>
<li>Stop optimization when the budget expires, all declared quality objectives are achieved, or measured trials support convergence. If stopping early, explain the changes tried, the metric comparisons and the remaining limitations. Track remaining time through session status. At soft-budget expiry, finish or cancel already-started work and submit immediately; do not start another command, check or optimization round. If an MCP execution is running, use <code>poll</code> with its <code>execution_id</code> and the returned <code>next_offset</code> as <code>offset</code> until completion, or cancel it and wait for it to stop.</li>
<li>Finish or cancel outstanding work, restore your best candidate to the declared output path, and explicitly submit it before ending the session. With layout MCP <code>submit</code>, remove the <code>/workspace/</code> prefix from <code>output.path</code>; with the container interface, run <code>python -I /protocol/submit.py</code>. Parasitic extraction is also mandatory: it must produce a complete, electrically consistent netlist usable by the required post-layout simulations. A completed candidate rejection scores zero; infrastructure errors do not establish candidate failure.</p>
<p>Physical checks and the declared functional bounds must pass. Quality scoring imposes no fixed degradation allowance. Each <code>source_*</code> job and its corresponding candidate extracted circuit use exactly the same testbench, model resources, parameters, loads and measurement windows. Independent source observations define the 100-point electrical baseline without depending on the submitted GDS; reports retain every paired result.</p>
<p>Let x be the post-layout measurement and b the source-circuit measurement under the same conditions:</p>
<ul>
<li>Ratio normalization: maximize with q = x/b and minimize with q = b/x. When a numerical scale s is declared, use (x+s)/(b+s) and its reciprocal, respectively. The scale handles zero values as a normalization floor; it is neither an allowed degradation nor an acceptance bound.</li>
<li>Amplitude in dB: maximize with q = 10^((x-b)/20) and minimize with its reciprocal.</li>
<li>Target normalization: q = 1/(1+abs(x-b)/s), where s is the declared physical scale. Signed or zero-valued operating points are not divided directly.</li>
</ul>
<p>Use <code>layout</code> scoring. Each metric takes its worst paired quality q, and the total score is S = 100 * product(min(1, q_i) ** w_i), with area quality q_area = area_reference / candidate_functional_area. The weights below sum to 1; metric dimensions describe measurements and do not determine weights. Physical, extraction, or functional rejection scores zero; other missing or invalid measurements leave the score unknown, and zero-weight observations must also be valid. Matching source-circuit performance at the reference area scores 100 points; each metric is capped at quality 1, so scores cannot exceed 100. Weights express quality tradeoffs rather than hard bounds.</p>
<p>The measurements below use the supplied SPICE node and source names. <code>v(n)</code> is a node voltage; <code>i(V)</code> is current flowing into a voltage source, so delivered power uses a negative sign. <code>db(z)=20*log10(abs(z))</code>; <code>mag/abs</code> denote magnitude, and <code>cph</code> denotes continuous phase in radians. <code>find</code>, <code>when</code>, <code>from/to</code> and <code>rise/fall</code> follow the deck's interpolation, window and crossing conventions. SPICE suffixes p/n/u/m/meg/g mean pico/nano/micro/milli/mega/giga. Nested <code>avg</code>, <code>min</code>, <code>max</code>, <code>vecmin/vecmax</code> and <code>deriv</code> represent window averages, extrema and sampled derivatives. Measure each operating condition independently, pair it with the source result under that condition, and take the worst paired quality within each metric group.</p>
<p><code>loaded_ripple_v</code>, <code>released_ripple_v</code>, <code>startup_error_v</code>, <code>recovery_load_v</code>, <code>recovery_release_v</code>, <code>line_span_v</code>, <code>load_span_v</code> use <code>saturating_ratio</code>: q = 2(b+s)/(b+x+2s), where x is candidate error, b is paired source error and s is the positive scale below. Source equivalence gives q=1; improvement approaches the upper limit 2 with diminishing returns. s retains the declared units and numeric-floor meaning; normalize each condition before taking the worst quality, and apply other rules as declared.</p>
