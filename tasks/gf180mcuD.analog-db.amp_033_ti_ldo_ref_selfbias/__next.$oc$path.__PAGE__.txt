4:T376f,<h1 id="problem-self-biased-ldo-reference-amplifier-layout-task">Self-Biased LDO Reference Amplifier Layout Task</h1>
<h2 id="problem-objective">Objective</h2>
<p>Implement <code>amp_033_ti_ldo_ref_selfbias</code> as a GF180MCU D layout. Nine MOS devices form the fixed upstream two-stage Miller amplifier, with a matched PMOS mirror and diode-connected self-bias. External unity feedback tests the reference-buffer function; no ideal internal amplifier or bias servo is inserted.</p>
<h3 id="problem-optimization-objective">Optimization objective</h3>
<p>Maximize the declared quality score within the solve budget while satisfying all physical and necessary functional requirements. The first passing DRC, LVS and required electrical checks establish a feasible baseline. Continue improving the scored metrics from that baseline; a passing check or <code>task_success = true</code> does not mean that the quality objectives have been achieved.</p>
<h2 id="problem-inputs-and-interface">Inputs and Interface</h2>
<p><code>materials/circuit.cdl</code> is the authoritative physical circuit, <code>materials/circuit.spice</code> its native simulation-model wrapper, and <code>materials/testbench.spice</code> defines all measurements. Ordered ports are <code>vdd vout vinp vinn vss</code>, respectively supply, output, differential positive/negative inputs and ground; no external bias current is needed. Preserve connectivity and total device sizes; equivalent parallel fingering is allowed, but the amplifier topology must not be replaced.</p>
<h2 id="problem-operating-conditions">Operating Conditions</h2>
<p>Typical native GF180 6 V MOS models, 3.3 V supply, 27 C, statistics disabled.
Input common mode is 0.8 V. Both 1 pF and 10 pF output loads are evaluated.
DC uses unity feedback. AC uses a 1 TH feedback inductor and 1 F input shunt
from 1 Hz to 1 GHz, 100 points/decade. Transient replaces these fixture elements
with 1 pH and 1 aF: the input rises 10 mV at 1 us and falls at 3 us, each in
10 ns. The simulation ends at 5 us, with a 1 ns requested step.
These ideal fixture elements are external test apparatus, outside the DUT.</p>
<h2 id="problem-physical-requirements">Physical Requirements</h2>
<p>GDS database unit 0.001 um; maximum 10 MiB; top cell <code>amp_033_ti_ldo_ref_selfbias</code>.
Native standalone DRC includes geometry, connectivity, manufacturing grid,
dummy fill and antenna rules; chip density and seal-ring closure are excluded.
Native LVS must match devices, parameters and every named port. All device,
well, implant, contact, passive-marker and routing layers listed in the runtime
outline form the functional bounding rectangle; text annotations are excluded.
Maximum outline is 5000 by 1000 um. Labels belong on native Metal1 text.</p>
<p>Minimum channel lengths for 6 V devices are NMOS 0.70 um and PMOS 0.55 um. Upstream dimensions are rounded to 5 nm, increasing only lengths below minimum. Physical passives use 1 kohm/square poly and MIM-B between M4/M5 (2 fF/um2); long resistors are segmented in series and capacitors tiled in parallel. Geometry realizes nominal values; source/candidate use identical native models. The candidate determines MOS diffusion areas/perimeters and interconnect parasitics; Magic extracts nominal coupling capacitance and distributed resistance with zero omission threshold. Ideal well/substrate connections do not cover distributed substrate noise, and no inductance, RF/EM or statistical performance is claimed. Source/candidate observations must be finite and each operating point must pass functional checks; incomplete evaluation cannot establish success.</p>
<h2 id="problem-electrical-requirements-and-scoring">Electrical Requirements and Scoring</h2>
<p>Artifact, geometry, DRC, LVS and declared functional requirements must pass. Parasitic extraction is also mandatory: it must produce a complete, electrically consistent netlist usable by the required post-layout simulations. A completed candidate rejection scores zero; infrastructure errors do not establish candidate failure.</p>
<p>Physical checks and the declared functional bounds must pass. Quality scoring imposes no fixed degradation allowance. Each <code>source_*</code> job and its corresponding candidate extracted circuit use exactly the same testbench, model resources, parameters, loads and measurement windows. Independent source observations define the 100-point electrical baseline without depending on the submitted GDS; reports retain every paired result.</p>
<p>Let x be the post-layout measurement and b the source-circuit measurement under the same conditions:</p>
<ul>
<li>Ratio normalization: maximize with q = x/b and minimize with q = b/x. When a numerical scale s is declared, use (x+s)/(b+s) and its reciprocal, respectively. The scale handles zero values as a normalization floor; it is neither an allowed degradation nor an acceptance bound.</li>
<li>Amplitude in dB: maximize with q = 10^((x-b)/20) and minimize with its reciprocal.</li>
<li>Target normalization: q = 1/(1+abs(x-b)/s), where s is the declared physical scale. Signed or zero-valued operating points are not divided directly.</li>
</ul>
<p>Use <code>layout</code> scoring. Each metric takes its worst paired quality q, and the total score is S = 100 * product(min(1, q_i) ** w_i), with area quality q_area = area_reference / candidate_functional_area. The weights below sum to 1; metric dimensions describe measurements and do not determine weights. Physical, extraction, or functional rejection scores zero; other missing or invalid measurements leave the score unknown, and zero-weight observations must also be valid. Matching source-circuit performance at the reference area scores 100 points; each metric is capped at quality 1, so scores cannot exceed 100. Weights express quality tradeoffs rather than hard bounds.</p>
<p><code>tracking_error_v</code> uses <code>saturating_ratio</code>: q = 2(b+s)/(b+x+2s), where x is candidate error, b is paired source error, and s is the positive scale below. Source-equivalent error gives q=1; improvement approaches the upper limit 2 with diminishing returns. s retains the declared units and numeric-floor meaning. Normalize each condition before taking the worst quality; other normalization and functional rules follow their own declarations.</p>
