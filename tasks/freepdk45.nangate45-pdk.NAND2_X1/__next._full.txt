11:T43b9,<h1 id="problem-two-input-nand-layout-task">Two-Input NAND Layout Task</h1>
<h2 id="problem-objective">Objective</h2>
<p>Implement ZN = NOT (A1 AND A2) with the supplied VTL transistors, preserving authoritative netlist models, W/L, interface and connections; top cell is <code>NAND2_X1</code>.</p>
<h3 id="problem-optimization-objective">Optimization objective</h3>
<p>Maximize the declared quality score within the solve budget while satisfying all physical and necessary functional requirements. The first passing DRC, LVS and required electrical checks establish a feasible baseline. Continue improving the scored metrics from that baseline; a passing check or <code>task_success = true</code> does not mean that the quality objectives have been achieved.</p>
<h2 id="problem-inputs-and-interface">Inputs and Interface</h2>
<p>Ordered SPICE ports: <code>A1 A2 ZN VDD VSS</code>. Names are case-insensitive, but different names are not interchangeable. Inputs are this description, <code>materials/circuit.spice</code> and <code>materials/testbench.spice</code>. The reviewed resource package supplies models and extraction resources; reference geometry and qualification evidence are not solver inputs.</p>
<h2 id="problem-operating-conditions">Operating Conditions</h2>
<p>Use nominal FreePDK45 BSIM4 models at <strong>1.0 V and 27 C</strong>, with ground at 0 V. Every transistor has channel length 50 nm; widths and model types follow the netlist.</p>
<p>ZN load is 5 fF. Excite A1/A2 with 00, 01, 11, 10, 00, 10, 11, 01, 00, using 500 ps slots and 10 ps edges; sample +110 ps after each slot starts, covering the truth table and each single-input transition in both directions. Average DUT supply power over 0–4.4 ns.</p>
<p>The deck precisely defines stimuli and observation times, testing only through external ports. Validation covers these nominal conditions, excluding PVT, mismatch, array integration, substrate noise and manufacturing signoff.</p>
<h2 id="problem-physical-requirements">Physical Requirements</h2>
<p>Submit a nonempty GDSII no larger than 10 MiB. Enable every check in the supplied KLayout DRC deck, including grid and antenna checks, without waivers. Strict LVS checks named ports, models, W/L and physical well/contact connectivity, with library-specific implicit or global power connections disabled. The upstream deck does not implement its documented spacing rule for wells at different potentials and does not provide complete foundry signoff.</p>
<p>The hard functional-outline limit is 100 um × 100 um. Its bounding box includes all functional geometry on drawing layers 1/0–29/0: active regions, wells, implants, threshold markers, poly, contacts, ten metal layers and nine via layers. Area is scored in um2. Keep functional geometry on these layers, excluding pin-purpose and annotation geometry. Electrical labels use poly or metal drawing layers (9/0, 11/0, 13/0, …, 29/0); nonelectrical annotation text is not extracted.</p>
<p>Translation, equivalent hierarchy and internal instance renaming are allowed. LVS permits source/drain exchange and equivalent parallel devices; the candidate must still pass the same RC electrical evaluation. Preserve top-level port identities and explicit body connections.</p>
<p>The standard-cell functional frame has fixed height 1.63 um and width on a 0.01 um grid. Coordinates below are relative to its lower-left corner; global translation is allowed. Supply rails are continuous on metal1 and bound to their corresponding named supplies by LVS.</p>
<ul>
<li><code>VDD</code>: center y=1.515 um, thickness at least 0.07 um, extending from +0.115 um inside the left boundary to the right boundary −0.03 um.</li>
<li><code>VSS</code>: center y=0.115 um, thickness at least 0.07 um, with the same horizontal extent as above.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="problem-electrical-requirements-and-scoring">Electrical Requirements and Scoring</h2>
<p>Artifact, geometry, DRC, LVS and declared functional requirements must pass. Parasitic extraction is also mandatory: it must produce a complete, electrically consistent netlist usable by the required post-layout simulations. A completed candidate rejection scores zero; infrastructure errors do not establish candidate failure.</p>
<p>For <code>layout</code> scoring, cap every positively weighted raw quality at 1 before aggregation; the score ranges from 0 to 100. Reaching every weighted target scores 100, and no minimum score is required for validity.</p>
<p>Extract the candidate GDS only after file, DRC, LVS and geometry checks pass. Simulate its Magic RC netlist directly, with independent source simulation supplying the scoring baseline. Independent LVS first checks VTG/VTL device types, then uses the matching extraction configuration. Extraction includes poly/metal sheet resistance, contacts/vias, geometry-dependent coupling and ground capacitance, and device junction areas/perimeters. Wells use lumped connectivity; BSIM4 describes device and junction behavior. Interconnect RC uses estimated coefficients and native capacitance placement from the fixed community FreePDK45 Magic technology. The adapter binds VTG/VTL models, preserves annotation layers and corrects dimension-unit conversion. This is a predictive approximation without field-solver signoff accuracy.</p>
<p>Physical checks and the declared functional bounds must pass. Each <code>source_*</code> job and its corresponding candidate extracted circuit use exactly the same testbench, model resources, parameters, loads and measurement windows. Electrical quality uses the paired pre-layout observations to score preservation of the fixed circuit's performance. Reports retain every source/candidate pair.</p>
<p>Let x be the post-layout measurement and b its same-condition pre-layout measurement:</p>
<ul>
<li>Ratio normalization: maximize with q = x/b and minimize with q = b/x. When a numerical scale s is declared, use (x+s)/(b+s) and its reciprocal, respectively. The scale handles zero values as a normalization floor; it is neither an allowed degradation nor an acceptance bound.</li>
<li>Amplitude in dB: maximize with q = 10^((x-b)/20) and minimize with its reciprocal.</li>
<li>Target normalization: q = 1/(1+abs(x-b)/s), where s is the declared physical scale. Signed or zero-valued operating points are not divided directly.</li>
</ul>
<p>Use <code>layout</code> scoring. First compute each metric's raw quality q from the worst paired condition, with raw area quality q_area = area_target / candidate_functional_area. Credit c_i = min(1, q_i) for every positively weighted metric, then compute S = 100 * product(c_i ** w_i). The weights sum to 1 and the score is bounded by 100. An improvement beyond one target cannot compensate for missing another target. Physical, extraction, or functional rejection scores zero; other missing or invalid measurements leave the score unknown, and zero-weight observations must also be valid. Reaching every positively weighted target scores 100; a feasible reference may score far below 100. Weights express quality tradeoffs, not acceptance bounds.</p>

<p>The area scoring target is <strong>0.8965 um2</strong>. It is a continuous quality goal, not a hard limit, process minimum or a percentage of the current feasibility witness.</p>
<p>The 0.8965 um2 target is an expert engineering estimate for 0.55 um width at the required 1.63 um height, on the 0.01 um width grid. A conventional unfolded two-PMOS row needs about 0.555 um width after contacts and well enclosure. The target therefore anticipates compact folding or rotation with shared diffusion: two 0.315 um fingers can preserve each 0.630 um PMOS width, while the 0.415 um NMOS width plus two 0.055 um well enclosures occupies 0.525 um before local access margin. Contacts, physical body ties, input/output access and both prescribed rails must fit in the full cell frame. This is a demanding engineering budget, not a demonstrated optimum, acceptance bound or prescribed layout topology. Propagation delay carries 45%, area 35% and output transition 20%. Timing and slew expose separate effects of critical-node routing; supply power remains diagnostic. All source pairs, logic states and prescribed rails remain checked.</p>
<p>The area objective and electrical normalization introduce no additional functional cutoffs. Each delay or transition is normalized against its matching pre-layout path and edge before taking the worst quality.</p>
<p>The capability coefficient is <strong>1</strong>, independent of this task's reference-normalized score.</p>
<h3 id="problem-standard-cell-design-guidance">Standard-cell design guidance</h3>
<p>You may plan device rows, supply rails and signal access before routing. Represent pull-up/pull-down connections as transistor-edge graphs and explore compatible Euler paths and alternative orders to share diffusion and reduce breaks. Choose a legal arrangement considering routing length, parasitics and signal access. Keep fixed netlist sizes, models and connections; do not optimize sizing.</p>
<p>Use the declared row height, grid and supply-rail geometry. Keep local routing compact and signal pins accessible, avoiding routing layers with no benefit. Check DRC and named-port LVS, then extract the candidate and compare transitions in both directions and power against source simulation; iterate using parasitic measurements. These methods are optional and do not prescribe an algorithm or reference layout.</p>
<p>These methods refer to Sections 2–3 of Xu et al., <em>Standard Cell Library Design and Optimization Methodology for ASAP7 PDK</em> (https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.11396). Its FinFET sizing, track count and process-specific dimensions do not apply to this task.</p>

<h2 id="problem-tools-and-submission">Tools and Submission</h2>
<p>Soft solve budget: <strong>1 hour</strong>.</p>
<p>Read <code>/protocol/task.json</code> for input/output paths, constraints and the <code>resources</code> object; <code>/protocol/harness.json</code> describes the active check and submission interface.</p>
<p>Use the reviewed FreePDK45 KLayout, Magic and ngspice resources in the <code>resources</code> field of <code>/protocol/task.json</code>. Magic RC extraction uses <code>/resources/support/magic-vtl/freepdk45.tech</code>, including model and unit adaptation. <code>/protocol/task.json</code> supplies consistent constraints, measurements and scoring; <code>/task</code> contains only declared inputs. Write <code>/workspace/output/final.gds</code>, then run <code>python -I /protocol/submit.py</code> to submit explicitly. <h3 id="problem-solve-workflow">Solve workflow</h3>
<ol>
<li>Read the declared circuit and materials, <code>/protocol/task.json</code> for paths, constraints and reviewed resources, and <code>/protocol/harness.json</code> for capabilities and helper commands. Task inputs under <code>/task</code> and resources under <code>/resources</code> are read-only; construct the layout in <code>/workspace</code> with the declared tools. With layout MCP, paths and commands refer to the remote EDA workspace. Create your own implementation with real devices, interconnect and pins, using your own tool loop without delegating to other agents; reference layouts and author code are not solver inputs.</li>
<li>Inspect <code>resources.external</code> before using external tools. Each command starts a fresh shell: when tool modules are declared, source <code>/usr/share/Modules/init/bash</code> and load the selected tool's modules; export its declared <code>tool_environment</code> settings in that same command. Repeat as needed and use batch or headless operation. Local manuals, command references, and release notes may be available within the declared EDA installation and PDK resource directories. Locate these resources through <code>/protocol/task.json</code> and consult the documentation for the installed tool version when diagnosing unfamiliar syntax or tool failures.</li>
<li>Generate an initial candidate and submit a snapshot early. When <code>process-feedback</code> is available, check the current GDS with layout MCP <code>check</code> or <code>python -I /protocol/process_check.py</code> before the final submission. This runs the final evaluator's complete plan and returns engineering diagnostics and candidate measurements, without scores or normalized quality factors. Checks have no count limit and consume the same wall-clock budget. Inspect the physical and functional results and all scored metrics; successful tool execution alone does not establish requirement satisfaction. Read paginated diagnostics with <code>report</code> when using MCP. Local exploratory checks do not replace this complete check.</li>
<li>Use the remaining time for measured improvements to the declared quality objectives. Identify the limiting scored metrics, make targeted layout changes, regenerate and check the candidate, and compare measurements with the previous candidate and the stated scoring targets and trade-offs. Preserve the best valid candidate and revert changes that worsen the overall objective. Repeat while time and useful improvements remain; passing validity checks alone is not a stopping condition.</li>
<li>Stop optimization when the budget expires, all declared quality objectives are achieved, or measured trials support convergence. If stopping early, explain the changes tried, the metric comparisons and the remaining limitations. Track remaining time through session status. At soft-budget expiry, finish or cancel already-started work and submit immediately; do not start another command, check or optimization round. If an MCP execution is running, use <code>poll</code> with its <code>execution_id</code> and the returned <code>next_offset</code> as <code>offset</code> until completion, or cancel it and wait for it to stop.</li>
<li>Finish or cancel outstanding work, restore your best candidate to the declared output path, and explicitly submit it before ending the session. With layout MCP <code>submit</code>, remove the <code>/workspace/</code> prefix from <code>output.path</code>; with the container interface, run <code>python -I /protocol/submit.py</code>. Wait for the receipt: only the last accepted snapshot is judged, and writing a file alone is not submission. Acceptance confirms delivery; the framework independently evaluates validity and quality after the session.</li>
</ol>
