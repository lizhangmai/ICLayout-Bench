4:T5e03,<h1 id="problem-two-stage-fully-differential-ota-with-rc-common-mode-feedback-layout-task">Two-Stage Fully Differential OTA with RC Common-Mode Feedback Layout Task</h1>
<h2 id="problem-objective">Objective</h2>
<p>Implement <code>amp_023_fer_fd2s</code>: an NMOS-input folded first stage, two NMOS
common-source output stages, dual series R/C compensation and transistor
common-mode feedback sensing both outputs through physical R/C arms. Preserve
all device groups and validate both differential and common-mode behavior in
the specified unity differential feedback connection.</p>
<h3 id="problem-optimization-objective">Optimization objective</h3>
<p>Maximize the declared quality score within the solve budget while satisfying all physical and necessary functional requirements. The first passing DRC, LVS and required electrical checks establish a feasible baseline. Continue improving the scored metrics from that baseline; a passing check or <code>task_success = true</code> does not mean that the quality objectives have been achieved.</p>
<h2 id="problem-inputs-and-interface">Inputs and Interface</h2>
<ul>
<li><code>materials/circuit.cdl</code>: authoritative physical circuit for native LVS.</li>
<li><code>materials/circuit.spice</code>: equivalent process-model simulator representation.</li>
<li><code>materials/testbench.spice</code>: nominal-temperature stimuli, differential feedback and measurements.</li>
<li><code>materials/performance_85c.spice</code>: the same testbench at 85 °C for the selected temperature-stress subset.</li>
</ul>
<p>Ordered ports are <code>vinp vinn voutp voutn vdd vss ibias vcmr</code>.
VINP/VINN are differential inputs; VOUTP/VOUTN are differential outputs;
VDD/VSS are supply/return; IBIAS receives 20 uA from VDD; VCMR receives the
common-mode reference relative to VSS. The bias mirrors and common-mode
controller stay in the DUT. Do not replace the internal controller with an
ideal servo or omit physical passive devices.</p>
<p>Retain all 28 MOS groups' fixed W/L/m (50 individual transistors), models and
connectivity. Equivalent parallel fingering/native merging is allowed if all
checks pass. NMOS and resistor bodies connect through the explicit substrate
tap to VSS; PMOS bodies connect through the well tap to VDD. Keep supplies,
body connections and ordered ports faithful to the circuit.</p>
<p>Each output compensation path has a W=1 um, L=0.96 um <code>rhigh</code> (nominally
1.52 kohm at 27 C) between <code>o1a</code>/<code>o1b</code> and <code>za</code>/<code>zb</code>, followed by two parallel
25.85 × 25.85 um <code>cap_cmim</code> units to the corresponding output (2.01294 pF).
Each output-to-<code>vsen</code> sensing arm contains eight series W=1 um, L=18 um
<code>rhigh</code> segments (nominally 205.28 kohm total), in parallel with a
9.95 × 9.95 um MIM (150.096 fF). Resistors use m=1, b=0. These internal
devices are separate from external output loads; they must be physically
implemented with the same terminal mapping. Internal nodes are not bias ports.</p>
<h2 id="problem-operating-conditions">Operating Conditions</h2>
<p>Use typical MOS, capacitor and resistor models, 27 C, 1.2 V supply, 20 uA
from VDD to IBIAS, and input common mode 0.5 V. Each output has an external
1, 5 or 10 pF load. For each load, evaluate paired positive and negative
stimuli: <code>step_v</code> is +0.1 or −0.1 V and <code>kick_a</code> is respectively +100 or
−100 uA. All six conditions must pass.</p>
<p>Define <code>d=VOUTP−VOUTN</code> and <code>c=(VOUTP+VOUTN)/2</code>. The external differential
feedback apparatus drives <code>VINP=0.5+(VSIG−d)/2</code> and
<code>VINN=0.5−(VSIG−d)/2</code>, through zero-volt series probes. It holds input
common mode and closes differential unity feedback. It does not force output
common mode: the actual DUT controller, its two R/C sense arms and actual
output stages close that loop.</p>
<p>At DC, VSIG=0 and VCMR=0.75 V. Differential AC uses opposite +0.5/−0.5 V
series probe excitations and sweeps 1 Hz–1 GHz at 150 points/decade. Measure
<code>A=d/(VINP−VINN)</code>; its sign is retained. Low-frequency gain is dB magnitude
at 1 Hz; unity frequency is the first falling 0 dB crossing; phase margin is
180 degrees plus continuous phase there, without subtracting an arbitrary
phase offset. A missing crossing is an error. This voltage-injection return
ratio is scoped to the high-impedance input and declared unity feedback.</p>
<p>Common-mode AC sets both series probe AC magnitudes to zero and VCMR AC=1 V,
keeping differential feedback closed. Sweep 1 Hz–1 GHz at 100 points/decade.
Measure <code>abs(c/VCMR)</code> at 1 Hz and its maximum over the complete sweep.
This is closed-loop common-mode tracking/peaking, not an internal common-mode
loop phase-margin measurement.</p>
<p>The transient sequence runs 0–8 us using Gear order 2 with maximum step 1 ns.
Use <code>rshunt=1e12</code>, <code>reltol=1e-5</code>, <code>abstol=1e-13</code>, <code>vntol=1e-8</code>.
VSIG rises from zero to <code>step_v</code> during 1–1.005 us, holds for 4 us, and
returns during 5.005–5.010 us. VCMR rises 0.75→0.8 V during 3–3.005 us,
holds for 1 us, and returns during 4.005–4.010 us. Thus reference tracking
is tested while a nonzero differential output is present. Equal current
sources inject <code>kick_a</code> into both outputs during a 6–6.060 us pulse:
5 ns rise, 50 ns hold and 5 ns fall. Negative <code>kick_a</code> withdraws current.
The pulse periods are 20 us; only this finite 8 us sequence is measured.
No startup initialization is imposed; transient starts from the solved DC point.</p>
<p>A selected phase-margin and CMFB subset repeats at 85 °C with the same TT device models, supply, references, loads and step/kick conditions. This checks temperature sensitivity only; no process-corner, mismatch or yield result is claimed.</p>
<h2 id="problem-physical-requirements">Physical Requirements</h2>
<p>Submit GDSII top cell <code>amp_023_fer_fd2s</code>, at most 10 MiB. Pass native IHP
main/maximal DRC without waivers (standalone scope, density/antenna disabled),
strict named-port LVS with physical taps, and a 1200 × 400 um functional
outline. Include all device, contact and routing drawing layers enumerated by
the runtime outline constraint. Pin-purpose and annotation layers do not
contribute to bounding-box area and cannot conceal functional geometry.</p>
<p>Post-layout measurements must consume the candidate-derived Magic RC netlist,
including physical passives, transistor junction geometry and interconnect.
The extraction configuration subdivides Magic's initial grid by two before
GDS import and imports its isolated copy with <code>gds_readonly=false</code>; the submitted
GDS remains immutable. No extraction error is waived. Device parameters,
geometry and port connectivity must survive extraction. Magic idealizes
well/substrate taps; source simulation retains their finite process models.
Distributed substrate resistance/noise and fabrication signoff are outside scope.</p>
<h2 id="problem-electrical-requirements-and-scoring">Electrical Requirements and Scoring</h2>
<p>Artifact, geometry, DRC, LVS and declared functional requirements must pass. Parasitic extraction is also mandatory: it must produce a complete, electrically consistent netlist usable by the required post-layout simulations. A completed candidate rejection scores zero; infrastructure errors do not establish candidate failure.</p>
<p>For <code>layout</code> scoring, cap every positively weighted raw quality at 1 before aggregation; the score ranges from 0 to 100. Reaching every weighted target scores 100, and no minimum score is required for validity.</p>
<p>Physical checks and the declared functional bounds must pass. Quality scoring imposes no fixed degradation allowance. Each <code>source_*</code> job and its corresponding candidate extracted circuit use exactly the same testbench, model resources, parameters, loads and measurement windows. Independent source observations remain required evidence. The explicit quality targets below supply scoring anchors; otherwise the paired source value is used. Reports retain every paired result.</p>
<p>Let x be the post-layout measurement and b the declared quality target, or the same-condition source measurement when no target is declared:</p>
<ul>
<li>Ratio normalization: maximize with q = x/b and minimize with q = b/x. When a numerical scale s is declared, use (x+s)/(b+s) and its reciprocal, respectively. The scale handles zero values as a normalization floor; it is neither an allowed degradation nor an acceptance bound.</li>
<li>Amplitude in dB: maximize with q = 10^((x-b)/20) and minimize with its reciprocal.</li>
<li>Target normalization: q = 1/(1+abs(x-b)/s), where s is the declared physical scale. Signed or zero-valued operating points are not divided directly.</li>
</ul>
<p>Use <code>layout</code> scoring. First compute each metric's raw quality q from the worst paired condition, with raw area quality q_area = area_target / candidate_functional_area. Credit c_i = min(1, q_i) for every positively weighted metric, then compute S = 100 * product(c_i ** w_i). The weights sum to 1 and the score is bounded by 100. An improvement beyond one target cannot compensate for missing another target. Physical, extraction, or functional rejection scores zero; other missing or invalid measurements leave the score unknown, and zero-weight observations must also be valid. Reaching every positively weighted target scores 100; a feasible reference may score far below 100. Weights express quality tradeoffs, not acceptance bounds.</p>
<p>Static common-mode accuracy is measured diagnostically, using three independently solved
DC equilibria for each load/polarity condition. Dynamic common-mode recovery is
measured relative to the corresponding circuit equilibrium, not the ideal
reference, so a fixed offset cannot earn repeated recovery rewards. The return
window ends at 4.95 us, before the differential input returns. The 100 uV scale
for static common-mode error, recovery and coupling equals 0.2% of the 50 mV
reference step; it declares useful accuracy, not a pass/fail tolerance or a
claim of process robustness. The original typical-model, 27 C, 1.2 V scope remains.</p>
<p>The measurements below use the supplied SPICE node and source names. <code>v(n)</code> is a node voltage; <code>i(V)</code> is current flowing into a voltage source, so delivered power uses a negative sign. <code>db(z)=20*log10(abs(z))</code>; <code>mag/abs</code> denote magnitude, and <code>cph</code> denotes continuous phase in radians. <code>find</code>, <code>when</code>, <code>from/to</code> and <code>rise/fall</code> follow the deck's interpolation, window and crossing conventions. SPICE suffixes p/n/u/m/meg/g mean pico/nano/micro/milli/mega/giga. Nested <code>avg</code>, <code>min</code>, <code>max</code>, <code>vecmin/vecmax</code> and <code>deriv</code> represent window averages, extrema and sampled derivatives. Measure each operating condition independently, pair it with the source result under that condition, and take the worst paired quality within each metric group.</p>
<p>For metrics declaring <code>saturating_ratio</code>, q = 2(b+s)/(b+x+2*s), using the quality anchor b and the positive scale s in the metric’s units. Equality to the anchor gives raw quality 1; improvements approach raw quality 2 but credited quality stays at 1. Normalize each condition before selecting the worst quality. These scales and targets do not add acceptance cutoffs.</p>

<h2 id="problem-tools-and-submission">Tools and Submission</h2>
<p>Soft solve budget: <strong>1.5 hours</strong>.</p>
<p>Discover task inputs, frozen requirements, reviewed resources and harness
feedback through <code>/protocol/task.json</code>, the <code>resources</code> field of <code>/protocol/task.json</code> and
<code>/protocol/harness.json</code>. Use the supplied IHP primitives and KLayout, Magic
and ngspice. Write <code>/workspace/output/final.gds</code> and explicitly submit using
the harness protocol. Reference GDS, source records and host configuration
remain outside standard solver inputs.</p>
<h3 id="problem-solve-workflow">Solve workflow</h3>
<ol>
<li>Read the declared circuit and materials, <code>/protocol/task.json</code> for paths, constraints and reviewed resources, and <code>/protocol/harness.json</code> for capabilities and helper commands. Task inputs under <code>/task</code> and resources under <code>/resources</code> are read-only; construct the layout in <code>/workspace</code> with the declared tools. With layout MCP, paths and commands refer to the remote EDA workspace. Create your own implementation with real devices, interconnect and pins, using your own tool loop without delegating to other agents; reference layouts and author code are not solver inputs.</li>
<li>Inspect <code>resources.external</code> before using external tools. Each command starts a fresh shell: when tool modules are declared, source <code>/usr/share/Modules/init/bash</code> and load the selected tool's modules; export its declared <code>tool_environment</code> settings in that same command. Repeat as needed and use batch or headless operation. Local manuals, command references, and release notes may be available within the declared EDA installation and PDK resource directories. Locate these resources through <code>/protocol/task.json</code> and consult the documentation for the installed tool version when diagnosing unfamiliar syntax or tool failures.</li>
<li>Generate an initial candidate and submit a snapshot early. When <code>process-feedback</code> is available, check the current GDS with layout MCP <code>check</code> or <code>python -I /protocol/process_check.py</code> before the final submission. This runs the final evaluator's complete plan and returns engineering diagnostics and candidate measurements, without scores or normalized quality factors. Checks have no count limit and consume the same wall-clock budget. Inspect the physical and functional results and all scored metrics; successful tool execution alone does not establish requirement satisfaction. Read paginated diagnostics with <code>report</code> when using MCP. Local exploratory checks do not replace this complete check.</li>
<li>Use the remaining time for measured improvements to the declared quality objectives. Identify the limiting scored metrics, make targeted layout changes, regenerate and check the candidate, and compare measurements with the previous candidate and the stated scoring targets and trade-offs. Preserve the best valid candidate and revert changes that worsen the overall objective. Repeat while time and useful improvements remain; passing validity checks alone is not a stopping condition.</li>

<p>Let x be the post-layout measurement and b the declared quality target, or the same-condition source measurement when no target is declared:</p>
<ul>
