11:T43ff,<h1 id="problem-two-stage-cmos-ota-layout-task">Two-Stage CMOS OTA Layout Task</h1>
<h2 id="problem-objective">Objective</h2>
<p>Create an IHP SG13G2 layout for the <code>two_stage_OTA_layout</code> two-stage CMOS
operational transconductance amplifier. Preserve the MOS and MIM devices,
dimensions, connectivity, well and substrate connections, and six-port
interface in the authoritative materials. The submitted GDS must pass the
physical checks, fit the functional outline, and meet the nominal DC-bias and
AC-response limits after candidate-derived RC extraction.</p>
<h3 id="problem-optimization-objective">Optimization objective</h3>
<p>Maximize the declared quality score within the solve budget while satisfying all physical and necessary functional requirements. The first passing DRC, LVS and required electrical checks establish a feasible baseline. Continue improving the scored metrics from that baseline; a passing check or <code>task_success = true</code> does not mean that the quality objectives have been achieved.</p>

<h2 id="problem-physical-requirements">Physical Requirements</h2>
<p>Submit a readable GDSII file with a nonempty <code>two_stage_OTA_layout</code> top cell. The
file must be at most 10 MiB (10,485,760 bytes) and must contain the complete
hierarchy needed by the target cell.</p>
<p>Use the pinned SG13G2 main rules and additional maximal DRC rules in deep mode, with density and antenna checks disabled and no waivers. LVS uses the current <code>lvs-upstream.json</code> configuration and strictly matches named ports against <code>materials/circuit.cdl</code>, preserving each port's independent conductor, connections and device parameters; port names are case insensitive.</p>
<p>The functional outline is the recursive bounding box of polygons on the following SG13G2 datatype-0 layers, including child cells and every routing layer:</p>
<p><code>1, 3, 5, 6, 7, 8, 10, 11, 13, 14, 19, 24, 26, 28, 29, 30, 31, 32, 33, 35, 36, 40, 44, 46, 49, 50, 51, 52, 53, 55, 58, 66, 67, 90, 101, 111, 125, 126, 128, 129, 133, 134, 139, 152</code>.</p>
<p>The maximum width is 80 µm and the maximum height is
50 µm, measured along the submitted X and Y axes.
Functional area is width multiplied by height. Text, annotation and filler
layers are excluded. The area score below uses this same functional footprint.</p>
<p>After the physical and geometry gates pass, Magic extracts distributed wire
resistance and layout capacitance from the submitted GDS. Device merging and
resistor-network simplification are disabled. The compact-device extraction
boundary represents MOS bodies at ideal model rails; explicit taps remain in the
physical LVS netlist but are not emitted as extracted tap elements. Substrate
sheet and tap resistance as extracted quantities, body coupling, and noise are
outside the declared scope. The finite source tap elements are covered by the
same-condition source calibration and are not treated as additional post-layout
requirements.</p>
<h2 id="problem-electrical-requirements-and-scoring">Electrical Requirements and Scoring</h2>
<p>Artifact, geometry, DRC, LVS and declared functional requirements must pass. Parasitic extraction is also mandatory: it must produce a complete, electrically consistent netlist usable by the required post-layout simulations. A completed candidate rejection scores zero; infrastructure errors do not establish candidate failure.</p>
<p>Physical checks and the declared functional bounds must pass. Quality scoring imposes no fixed degradation allowance. Each <code>source_*</code> job and its corresponding candidate extracted circuit use exactly the same testbench, model resources, parameters, loads and measurement windows. Independent source observations define the 100-point electrical baseline without depending on the submitted GDS; reports retain every paired result.</p>
<p>Let x be the post-layout measurement and b the source-circuit measurement under the same conditions:</p>
<ul>
<li>Ratio normalization: maximize with q = x/b and minimize with q = b/x. When a numerical scale s is declared, use (x+s)/(b+s) and its reciprocal, respectively. The scale handles zero values as a normalization floor; it is neither an allowed degradation nor an acceptance bound.</li>
<li>Amplitude in dB: maximize with q = 10^((x-b)/20) and minimize with its reciprocal.</li>
<li>Target normalization: q = 1/(1+abs(x-b)/s), where s is the declared physical scale. Signed or zero-valued operating points are not divided directly.</li>
</ul>
<p>Use <code>layout</code> scoring. Each metric takes its worst paired quality q, and the total score is S = 100 * product(min(1, q_i) ** w_i), with area quality q_area = area_reference / candidate_functional_area. The weights below sum to 1; metric dimensions describe measurements and do not determine weights. Physical, extraction, or functional rejection scores zero; other missing or invalid measurements leave the score unknown, and zero-weight observations must also be valid. Matching source-circuit performance at the reference area scores 100 points; each metric is capped at quality 1, so scores cannot exceed 100. Weights express quality tradeoffs rather than hard bounds.</p>
<p>The measurements below use the supplied SPICE node and source names. <code>v(n)</code> is a node voltage; <code>i(V)</code> is current flowing into a voltage source, so delivered power uses a negative sign. <code>db(z)=20*log10(abs(z))</code>; <code>mag/abs</code> denote magnitude, and <code>cph</code> denotes continuous phase in radians. <code>find</code>, <code>when</code>, <code>from/to</code> and <code>rise/fall</code> follow the deck's interpolation, window and crossing conventions. SPICE suffixes p/n/u/m/meg/g mean pico/nano/micro/milli/mega/giga. Nested <code>avg</code>, <code>min</code>, <code>max</code>, <code>vecmin/vecmax</code> and <code>deriv</code> represent window averages, extrema and sampled derivatives. Measure each operating condition independently, pair it with the source result under that condition, and take the worst paired quality within each metric group.</p>

<h2 id="problem-tools-and-submission">Tools and Submission</h2>
<p>Soft solve budget: <strong>3 hours</strong>.</p>
<p>See the <code>resources</code> field of <code>/protocol/task.json</code> and <code>/protocol/harness.json</code> for runtime and supporting resources; the working directory is <code>/workspace</code>. KLayout checks files, DRC, LVS and geometry, Magic extracts RC, and ngspice runs decks with reviewed SG13G2 models. The evaluator supplies tools and PDK specified by the contract toolchain.</p>
<p>For pre-layout simulation, use <code>materials/circuit.spice</code> as <code>dut.spice</code> beside
the supplied testbench, with the model paths and settings exposed by the runtime
resource bundle. For the submitted layout, the evaluator supplies the
candidate-derived extracted netlist. If the harness declares
<code>process-feedback</code>, use the full-plan check command
<code>python -I /protocol/process_check.py</code>; the final evaluation remains independent.</p>
<p>Write the result to <code>/workspace/output/final.gds</code>, run <code>python -I /protocol/submit.py</code> and wait for a submission receipt. Solver netlists, waveforms and measurements cannot replace independent evaluator inputs.</p>
<h3 id="problem-solve-workflow">Solve workflow</h3>
<ol>
<li>Read the declared circuit and materials, <code>/protocol/task.json</code> for paths, constraints and reviewed resources, and <code>/protocol/harness.json</code> for capabilities and helper commands. Task inputs under <code>/task</code> and resources under <code>/resources</code> are read-only; construct the layout in <code>/workspace</code> with the declared tools. With layout MCP, paths and commands refer to the remote EDA workspace. Create your own implementation with real devices, interconnect and pins, using your own tool loop without delegating to other agents; reference layouts and author code are not solver inputs.</li>
<li>Inspect <code>resources.external</code> before using external tools. Each command starts a fresh shell: when tool modules are declared, source <code>/usr/share/Modules/init/bash</code> and load the selected tool's modules; export its declared <code>tool_environment</code> settings in that same command. Repeat as needed and use batch or headless operation. Local manuals, command references, and release notes may be available within the declared EDA installation and PDK resource directories. Locate these resources through <code>/protocol/task.json</code> and consult the documentation for the installed tool version when diagnosing unfamiliar syntax or tool failures.</li>
<li>Generate an initial candidate and submit a snapshot early. When <code>process-feedback</code> is available, check the current GDS with layout MCP <code>check</code> or <code>python -I /protocol/process_check.py</code> before the final submission. This runs the final evaluator's complete plan and returns engineering diagnostics and candidate measurements, without scores or normalized quality factors. Checks have no count limit and consume the same wall-clock budget. Inspect the physical and functional results and all scored metrics; successful tool execution alone does not establish requirement satisfaction. Read paginated diagnostics with <code>report</code> when using MCP. Local exploratory checks do not replace this complete check.</li>
<li>Use the remaining time for measured improvements to the declared quality objectives. Identify the limiting scored metrics, make targeted layout changes, regenerate and check the candidate, and compare measurements with the previous candidate and the stated scoring targets and trade-offs. Preserve the best valid candidate and revert changes that worsen the overall objective. Repeat while time and useful improvements remain; passing validity checks alone is not a stopping condition.</li>
<li>Stop optimization when the budget expires, all declared quality objectives are achieved, or measured trials support convergence. If stopping early, explain the changes tried, the metric comparisons and the remaining limitations. Track remaining time through session status. At soft-budget expiry, finish or cancel already-started work and submit immediately; do not start another command, check or optimization round. If an MCP execution is running, use <code>poll</code> with its <code>execution_id</code> and the returned <code>next_offset</code> as <code>offset</code> until completion, or cancel it and wait for it to stop.</li>
<li>Finish or cancel outstanding work, restore your best candidate to the declared output path, and explicitly submit it before ending the session. With layout MCP <code>submit</code>, remove the <code>/workspace/</code> prefix from <code>output.path</code>; with the container interface, run <code>python -I /protocol/submit.py</code>. Wait for the receipt: only the last accepted snapshot is judged, and writing a file alone is not submission. Acceptance confirms delivery; the framework independently evaluates validity and quality after the session.</li>
</ol>
