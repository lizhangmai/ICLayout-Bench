11:T40b0,<h1 id="problem-quadrature-voltage-controlled-oscillator-layout-task">Quadrature Voltage-Controlled Oscillator Layout Task</h1>
<h2 id="problem-objective">Objective</h2>
<p>Implement <code>vco</code> in GF180MCU variant D while preserving the supplied circuit, device dimensions and ordered interface. Four-phase oscillation, quadrature and frequency tuning at control voltages 1.65 V and 2.10 V, checked across typical-model temperature and output-load conditions.</p>
<h3 id="problem-optimization-objective">Optimization objective</h3>
<p>Maximize the declared quality score within the solve budget while satisfying all physical and necessary functional requirements. The first passing DRC, LVS and required electrical checks establish a feasible baseline. Continue improving the scored metrics from that baseline; a passing check or <code>task_success = true</code> does not mean that the quality objectives have been achieved.</p>
<h2 id="problem-inputs-and-interface">Inputs and Interface</h2>
<p>Inputs are this description, <code>materials/circuit.spice</code> (the authoritative circuit) and <code>materials/testbench.spice</code> (stimuli and measurements). Runtime task metadata supplies consistent frozen constraints, evaluation conditions and output declarations.</p>
<p>Ordered subcircuit and GDS interface: <code>GND, VDD, ctrl, out_0, out_90, out_180, out_270</code>.
GND/VDD: return/supply; ctrl: tuning voltage; out_0/out_90/out_180/out_270: quadrature outputs.</p>
<p>Preserve topology, total device widths, channel lengths, multiplicities, resistor dimensions and body connections. Equivalent hierarchy, diffusion sharing and parallel fingers are allowed when connectivity and electrical checks pass; preserve source models and dimensions without idealizing internal devices. Retain dummy devices even when all their terminals are shorted.</p>
<h2 id="problem-operating-conditions">Operating Conditions</h2>
<p>Use typical models at -20, 27 and 100 C. At each temperature run two independent copies of the submitted circuit at ctrl = 1.65 V and 2.10 V, with 25, 50 and 100 fF loads on each output. Each copy has its own ideal supply ramping from 0 V at 0 ns to 3.3 V at 1 ns. Simulate through 600 ns with a 0.02 ns requested step and characterize steady oscillation over 400–600 ns. This is a nine-condition temperature/load matrix, not a process-corner or statistical-yield qualification. Every applicable performance metric is aggregated at its worst paired condition; functional output swing is checked at all outputs, controls and conditions.</p>
<p>Use the reviewed typical primitive models with statistical variation disabled. Numerical parameters in the runtime simulation jobs supply the testbench's <code>parameters.spice</code>. Follow the delivered testbench for interpolation and detailed PWL definitions. Process corners, mismatch yield, noise, RF/EM and full-chip density closure are outside this task's qualification scope.</p>
<h2 id="problem-physical-requirements">Physical Requirements</h2>
<p>Submit a nonempty GDS with top cell <code>vco</code>, no larger than 10485760 bytes. The functional bounding box must fit within 700 × 240 um and includes relevant device and routing polygons, implants/wells, passive markers, contacts, vias and dummy routing. The complete layer/datatype set is:</p>
<p><code>[[5,0],[11,17],[11,39],[12,0],[13,17],[21,0],[22,0],[22,4],[24,0],[24,5],[30,0],[30,4],[31,0],[32,0],[33,0],[34,0],[34,3],[34,4],[34,5],[35,0],[36,0],[36,3],[36,4],[36,5],[37,0],[38,0],[40,0],[41,0],[42,0],[42,3],[42,4],[42,5],[46,0],[46,3],[46,4],[46,5],[49,0],[53,0],[53,3],[53,4],[53,5],[55,0],[62,0],[75,0],[80,5],[81,0],[81,3],[81,4],[81,5],[82,0],[86,17],[88,17],[96,1],[100,5],[100,7],[100,8],[108,5],[110,5],[110,11],[110,12],[110,13],[110,14],[110,15],[110,16],[111,5],[112,1],[115,5],[116,5],[117,5],[117,10],[118,5],[119,5],[122,5],[123,5],[124,5],[125,5],[127,5],[128,17],[137,5],[151,5],[152,5],[153,51],[166,5],[167,5],[173,5],[178,0],[183,0],[184,0],[185,0],[204,0],[210,0],[220,0],[226,0],[227,0],[241,0]]</code>.</p>
<p>Area excludes text and nonfunctional boundary layers 0/0 and 63/0; functional geometry must remain on functional layers. Pass the fixed GF180 D FEOL, BEOL, connectivity, grid and antenna checks without waivers. Use five metal layers, the 1.1 um top-metal option and the 1 kOhm/square high-resistance poly option. Chip density and seal-ring closure belong to higher-level integration.</p>
<p>LVS checks topology, dimensions, body terminals and all named top-level ports, case-insensitively. Physically implement supply and substrate/well contacts; do not short different nets through adjoining silicided diffusion. After validity and geometric checks pass, extract candidate devices and distributed interconnect RC, then simulate the extracted circuit. The substrate model is one equipotential body region with explicit well/body contacts, rather than a distributed silicon-substrate network. Unreliable extraction, missing ports or incomplete measurements cannot establish success.</p>
<h2 id="problem-electrical-requirements-and-scoring">Electrical Requirements and Scoring</h2>
<p>Artifact, geometry, DRC, LVS and declared functional requirements must pass. Parasitic extraction is also mandatory: it must produce a complete, electrically consistent netlist usable by the required post-layout simulations. A completed candidate rejection scores zero; infrastructure errors do not establish candidate failure.</p>
<p>For <code>layout</code> scoring, cap every positively weighted raw quality at 1 before aggregation; the score ranges from 0 to 100. Reaching every weighted target scores 100, and no minimum score is required for validity.</p>
<p>Physical checks and the declared functional bounds must pass. Quality scoring imposes no fixed degradation allowance. Each <code>source_*</code> job and its corresponding candidate extracted circuit use exactly the same testbench, model resources, parameters, loads and measurement windows. Independent source observations remain required evidence. The explicit quality targets below supply scoring anchors; otherwise the paired source value is used. Reports retain every paired result.</p>
<p>Let x be the post-layout measurement and b the declared quality target, or the same-condition source measurement when no target is declared:</p>
<ul>
<li>Ratio normalization: maximize with q = x/b and minimize with q = b/x. When a numerical scale s is declared, use (x+s)/(b+s) and its reciprocal, respectively. The scale handles zero values as a normalization floor; it is neither an allowed degradation nor an acceptance bound.</li>
<li>Amplitude in dB: maximize with q = 10^((x-b)/20) and minimize with its reciprocal.</li>
<li>Target normalization: q = 1/(1+abs(x-b)/s), where s is the declared physical scale. Signed or zero-valued operating points are not divided directly.</li>
</ul>
<p>Use <code>layout</code> scoring. First compute each metric's raw quality q from the worst paired condition, with raw area quality q_area = area_target / candidate_functional_area. Credit c_i = min(1, q_i) for every positively weighted metric, then compute S = 100 * product(c_i ** w_i). The weights sum to 1 and the score is bounded by 100. An improvement beyond one target cannot compensate for missing another target. Physical, extraction, or functional rejection scores zero; other missing or invalid measurements leave the score unknown, and zero-weight observations must also be valid. Reaching every positively weighted target scores 100; a feasible reference may score far below 100. Weights express quality tradeoffs, not acceptance bounds.</p>

<h3 id="problem-quality-targets-and-weights">Quality targets and weights</h3>
<p>The area quality target is <strong>250 um2</strong>, independent of the current feasibility witness. It is not a hard area limit or a process minimum.</p>
<p>The 250 um2 footprint carries 25%. The three relative phases together carry 45%, centered on exact quarter/half/three-quarter cycles with a one-degree deviation scale; a full-cycle scale hid useful routing asymmetry. The two frequencies retain temperature/load-specific source anchors with 5 and 10 MHz scales and 10% weight each. Tuning and supply power receive 5% each. Faster oscillation alone is not rewarded at the expense of the intended control law.</p>

<h2 id="problem-tools-and-submission">Tools and Submission</h2>
<p>Soft solve budget: <strong>1.5 hours</strong>.</p>
<p>Read <code>/protocol/task.json</code> for input/output paths, constraints and the <code>resources</code> object; <code>/protocol/harness.json</code> describes the active check and submission interface.</p>
<p>Discover inputs and reviewed GF180 resources through the runtime task, resource and submission protocols. Trusted feedback executes the declared physical and post-layout evaluation; source simulation only assists design. Write the final GDS to <code>/workspace/output/final.gds</code> and explicitly submit through the runtime protocol. File generation or a successful standalone simulation alone does not complete the task.</p>
<p>Use a GDS database unit of 0.001 um as required by GF180MCU DRC rules.</p>
<h3 id="problem-solve-workflow">Solve workflow</h3>
<ol>
<li>Read the declared circuit and materials, <code>/protocol/task.json</code> for paths, constraints and reviewed resources, and <code>/protocol/harness.json</code> for capabilities and helper commands. Task inputs under <code>/task</code> and resources under <code>/resources</code> are read-only; construct the layout in <code>/workspace</code> with the declared tools. With layout MCP, paths and commands refer to the remote EDA workspace. Create your own implementation with real devices, interconnect and pins, using your own tool loop without delegating to other agents; reference layouts and author code are not solver inputs.</li>
<li>Inspect <code>resources.external</code> before using external tools. Each command starts a fresh shell: when tool modules are declared, source <code>/usr/share/Modules/init/bash</code> and load the selected tool's modules; export its declared <code>tool_environment</code> settings in that same command. Repeat as needed and use batch or headless operation. Local manuals, command references, and release notes may be available within the declared EDA installation and PDK resource directories. Locate these resources through <code>/protocol/task.json</code> and consult the documentation for the installed tool version when diagnosing unfamiliar syntax or tool failures.</li>
<li>Generate an initial candidate and submit a snapshot early. When <code>process-feedback</code> is available, check the current GDS with layout MCP <code>check</code> or <code>python -I /protocol/process_check.py</code> before the final submission. This runs the final evaluator's complete plan and returns engineering diagnostics and candidate measurements, without scores or normalized quality factors. Checks have no count limit and consume the same wall-clock budget. Inspect the physical and functional results and all scored metrics; successful tool execution alone does not establish requirement satisfaction. Read paginated diagnostics with <code>report</code> when using MCP. Local exploratory checks do not replace this complete check.</li>
<li>Use the remaining time for measured improvements to the declared quality objectives. Identify the limiting scored metrics, make targeted layout changes, regenerate and check the candidate, and compare measurements with the previous candidate and the stated scoring targets and trade-offs. Preserve the best valid candidate and revert changes that worsen the overall objective. Repeat while time and useful improvements remain; passing validity checks alone is not a stopping condition.</li>
<li>Stop optimization when the budget expires, all declared quality objectives are achieved, or measured trials support convergence. If stopping early, explain the changes tried, the metric comparisons and the remaining limitations. Track remaining time through session status. At soft-budget expiry, finish or cancel already-started work and submit immediately; do not start another command, check or optimization round. If an MCP execution is running, use <code>poll</code> with its <code>execution_id</code> and the returned <code>next_offset</code> as <code>offset</code> until completion, or cancel it and wait for it to stop.</li>
<li>Finish or cancel outstanding work, restore your best candidate to the declared output path, and explicitly submit it before ending the session. With layout MCP <code>submit</code>, remove the <code>/workspace/</code> prefix from <code>output.path</code>; with the container interface, run <code>python -I /protocol/submit.py</code>. Wait for the receipt: only the last accepted snapshot is judged, and writing a file alone is not submission. Acceptance confirms delivery; the framework independently evaluates validity and quality after the session.</li>
</ol>
