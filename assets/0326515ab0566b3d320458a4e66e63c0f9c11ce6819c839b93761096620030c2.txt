# Physical NMOS Sample-and-Hold Layout Task

## Objective

Implement `smp_001_nmos_th`: a single-NMOS sampling switch with an internal
physical storage capacitor. Preserve the switch and stored-charge node, and
meet acquisition, turn-off pedestal, isolated-input feedthrough, hold drift
and reacquisition requirements at the specified input and temperature points.

### Optimization objective

Maximize the declared quality score within the solve budget while satisfying all physical and necessary functional requirements. The first passing DRC, LVS and required electrical checks establish a feasible baseline. Continue improving the scored metrics from that baseline; a passing check or `task_success = true` does not mean that the quality objectives have been achieved.

## Inputs and Interface

- `materials/circuit.cdl`: authoritative native physical circuit.
- `materials/circuit.spice`: equivalent simulator representation.
- `materials/testbench.spice`: independent stimuli and measurements.

The ordered interface is `vin clk vout vss`. VIN is driven through an external
1 kohm source resistance; CLK receives the external clock; VOUT is the storage
node. The source's unused VDD port is removed. There is no DUT supply-current
metric or added artificial supply connection. Tie VSS to ground.

Retain the `sg13_lv_nmos` with W=2 um, L=0.13 um, m=1 and drain/gate/source
connected to VIN/CLK/VOUT. Its body connects through the explicit VSS substrate
tap. One `cap_cmim` with W=L=18.2 um connects its top plate to VOUT and bottom
plate to VSS, nominally 499.772 fF at TT, 27 C. Preserve plate orientation.
The capacitor is internal physical storage, not a testbench load or an ideal
replacement. The native and simulator circuits include the same explicit tap.

Preserve models, sizes and connectivity. Equivalent parallel fingering is
permitted only when all physical and electrical checks pass. Do not add a
buffer, complementary switch, dummy cancellation device, hold-node servo,
external storage capacitor or other topology change.

## Operating Conditions

Use typical MOS and capacitor models at 27 and 85 C. CLK is externally driven
between 0 and 1.2 V, with 2 ns linear transitions. The ideal signal source SRC
drives VIN through 1 kohm. Qualify four sampled levels, `sample_v` = 0.1, 0.4,
0.6 and 0.7 V, at both temperatures. At each point the off-state input is
separately driven to `hold_v` = 0 or 1.2 V: sixteen conditions. These are
specified sample points, not rail-to-rail or continuous-range qualification.

The finite sequence starts from a solved DC operating point with SRC=0 and
CLK=1.2 V. SRC moves to `sample_v` during 20–22 ns. CLK falls during
200–202 ns, isolating the storage node. SRC moves to `hold_v` during
400–402 ns while CLK remains zero. SRC returns to `sample_v` during
11.2–11.202 us; CLK rises during 11.4–11.402 us. Stop at 11.7 us.
No output load, DC bias source or discharge resistor is attached to VOUT.

Use Gear order 2, KLU, maximum time step 2 ns, `gmin=1e-15`,
`rshunt=1e15`, `reltol=1e-6`, `abstol=1e-15` and `vntol=1e-9`.
The numerical shunt is not a specified physical retention load. Save SRC,
VIN, CLK, VOUT and both ideal voltage-source currents.

## Physical Requirements

Submit GDSII top cell `smp_001_nmos_th`, at most 10 MiB, within an 80 × 80 um
functional outline. Pass native IHP main/maximal DRC without waivers
(standalone scope with density/antenna disabled), strict named-port LVS,
functional geometry and candidate-derived Magic RC extraction. Functional
area includes the complete device/contact/routing layers enumerated by the
runtime outline; annotation and pin layers do not count.

Post-layout simulation must consume the submitted layout's extracted NMOS,
physical MIM, junction geometry and interconnect RC. The isolated Magic import
uses half-grid subdivision and `gds_readonly=false`; the submitted GDS remains
immutable. No diagnostic is waived. Source simulation retains the finite tap
model; Magic idealizes the tap connection. Distributed substrate noise and
fabrication signoff are outside this contract.

## Electrical Requirements and Scoring

Artifact, geometry, DRC, LVS and declared functional requirements must pass. Parasitic extraction is also mandatory: it must produce a complete, electrically consistent netlist usable by the required post-layout simulations. A completed candidate rejection scores zero; infrastructure errors do not establish candidate failure.

For `layout` scoring, cap every positively weighted raw quality at 1 before aggregation; the score ranges from 0 to 100. Reaching every weighted target scores 100, and no minimum score is required for validity.

Physical checks and the declared functional bounds must pass. Quality scoring imposes no fixed degradation allowance. Each `source_*` job and its corresponding candidate extracted circuit use exactly the same testbench, model resources, parameters, loads and measurement windows. Independent source observations remain required evidence. The explicit quality targets below supply scoring anchors; otherwise the paired source value is used. Reports retain every paired result.

Let x be the post-layout measurement and b the declared quality target, or the same-condition source measurement when no target is declared:

- Ratio normalization: maximize with q = x/b and minimize with q = b/x. When a numerical scale s is declared, use (x+s)/(b+s) and its reciprocal, respectively. The scale handles zero values as a normalization floor; it is neither an allowed degradation nor an acceptance bound.
- Amplitude in dB: maximize with q = 10^((x-b)/20) and minimize with its reciprocal.
- Target normalization: q = 1/(1+abs(x-b)/s), where s is the declared physical scale. Signed or zero-valued operating points are not divided directly.

Use `layout` scoring. First compute each metric's raw quality q from the worst paired condition, with raw area quality q_area = area_target / candidate_functional_area. Credit c_i = min(1, q_i) for every positively weighted metric, then compute S = 100 * product(c_i ** w_i). The weights sum to 1 and the score is bounded by 100. An improvement beyond one target cannot compensate for missing another target. Physical, extraction, or functional rejection scores zero; other missing or invalid measurements leave the score unknown, and zero-weight observations must also be valid. Reaching every positively weighted target scores 100; a feasible reference may score far below 100. Weights express quality tradeoffs, not acceptance bounds.

The measurements below use the supplied SPICE node and source names. `v(n)` is a node voltage; `i(V)` is current flowing into a voltage source, so delivered power uses a negative sign. `db(z)=20*log10(abs(z))`; `mag/abs` denote magnitude, and `cph` denotes continuous phase in radians. `find`, `when`, `from/to` and `rise/fall` follow the deck's interpolation, window and crossing conventions. SPICE suffixes p/n/u/m/meg/g mean pico/nano/micro/milli/mega/giga. Nested `avg`, `min`, `max`, `vecmin/vecmax` and `deriv` represent window averages, extrema and sampled derivatives. Measure each operating condition independently, pair it with the source result under that condition, and take the worst paired quality within each metric group.

For metrics declaring `saturating_ratio`, q = 2(b+s)/(b+x+2*s), using the quality anchor b and the positive scale s in the metric’s units. Equality to the anchor gives raw quality 1; improvements approach raw quality 2 but credited quality stays at 1. Normalize each condition before selecting the worst quality. These scales and targets do not add acceptance cutoffs.

### Quality targets and weights

The area quality target is **400 um2**. It is an integration budget, not a hard limit or a proven process minimum.

The fixed 18.2 um square storage plate occupies 331.24 um2. A 400 um2 integration budget leaves 68.76 um2 beyond that plate for enclosure, the sampling switch, body tap and routing and carries 30%. Held-value accuracy carries 25%; pedestal, off-state feedthrough, tracking and clock energy carry 10% each; reacquisition carries 5%. The 2.7 fJ positive clock-source energy budget leaves approximately 0.121 fJ, or 4.7%, above the worst source-circuit energy for clock routing and contacts. Hold drift remains a diagnostic decomposition of held-value error. The fixed-switch electrical targets retain their physical anchors; the worst of all 16 sample/temperature/off-state conditions determines each quality factor.

## Tools and Submission

Soft solve budget: **1 hour**.

Use `/protocol/task.json` (including its `resources` field) and `/protocol/harness.json`
for the frozen inputs, requirements, reviewed IHP primitives and harness
feedback. Work with the supplied KLayout, Magic and ngspice resources.
Write `/workspace/output/final.gds` and explicitly submit through the harness.
Reference GDS, source records and host configuration are not standard inputs.

### Solve workflow

1. Read the declared circuit and materials, `/protocol/task.json` for paths, constraints and reviewed resources, and `/protocol/harness.json` for capabilities and helper commands. Task inputs under `/task` and resources under `/resources` are read-only; construct the layout in `/workspace` with the declared tools. With layout MCP, paths and commands refer to the remote EDA workspace. Create your own implementation with real devices, interconnect and pins, using your own tool loop without delegating to other agents; reference layouts and author code are not solver inputs.
2. Inspect `resources.external` before using external tools. Each command starts a fresh shell: when tool modules are declared, source `/usr/share/Modules/init/bash` and load the selected tool's modules; export its declared `tool_environment` settings in that same command. Repeat as needed and use batch or headless operation. Local manuals, command references, and release notes may be available within the declared EDA installation and PDK resource directories. Locate these resources through `/protocol/task.json` and consult the documentation for the installed tool version when diagnosing unfamiliar syntax or tool failures.
3. Generate an initial candidate and submit a snapshot early. When `process-feedback` is available, check the current GDS with layout MCP `check` or `python -I /protocol/process_check.py` before the final submission. This runs the final evaluator's complete plan and returns engineering diagnostics and candidate measurements, without scores or normalized quality factors. Checks have no count limit and consume the same wall-clock budget. Inspect the physical and functional results and all scored metrics; successful tool execution alone does not establish requirement satisfaction. Read paginated diagnostics with `report` when using MCP. Local exploratory checks do not replace this complete check.
4. Use the remaining time for measured improvements to the declared quality objectives. Identify the limiting scored metrics, make targeted layout changes, regenerate and check the candidate, and compare measurements with the previous candidate and the stated scoring targets and trade-offs. Preserve the best valid candidate and revert changes that worsen the overall objective. Repeat while time and useful improvements remain; passing validity checks alone is not a stopping condition.
5. Stop optimization when the budget expires, all declared quality objectives are achieved, or measured trials support convergence. If stopping early, explain the changes tried, the metric comparisons and the remaining limitations. Track remaining time through session status. At soft-budget expiry, finish or cancel already-started work and submit immediately; do not start another command, check or optimization round. If an MCP execution is running, use `poll` with its `execution_id` and the returned `next_offset` as `offset` until completion, or cancel it and wait for it to stop.
6. Finish or cancel outstanding work, restore your best candidate to the declared output path, and explicitly submit it before ending the session. With layout MCP `submit`, remove the `/workspace/` prefix from `output.path`; with the container interface, run `python -I /protocol/submit.py`. Wait for the receipt: only the last accepted snapshot is judged, and writing a file alone is not submission. Acceptance confirms delivery; the framework independently evaluates validity and quality after the session.
