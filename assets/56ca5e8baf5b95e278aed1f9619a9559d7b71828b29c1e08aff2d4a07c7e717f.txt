# Eight-Bit R-2R DAC Core Layout Task

## Objective

Implement `r2r` in GF180MCU variant D while preserving the supplied circuit, device dimensions and ordered interface. Eight-bit passive voltage conversion with ideal external bit drivers and a 1 pF output load; ten transient codes plus the complete static 256-code transfer.

## Inputs and Interface

Inputs are this description, `materials/circuit.spice` (the authoritative circuit) and `materials/testbench.spice` (stimuli and measurements). Runtime task metadata supplies consistent frozen constraints, evaluation conditions and output declarations.

Ordered subcircuit and GDS interface: `b7, b1, b0, b2, b3, b4, b5, b6, out, VGND`.
b0–b7: ideal-driver bit inputs (b0 is LSB); out: analog output; VGND: substrate and ladder return.

Preserve topology, total device widths, channel lengths, multiplicities, resistor dimensions and body connections. Equivalent hierarchy, diffusion sharing and parallel fingers are allowed when connectivity and electrical checks pass; preserve source models and dimensions without idealizing internal devices. Retain dummy devices even when all their terminals are shorted.

## Operating Conditions

Use 3.3 V bit-driver levels and 27 C, with 1 pF from out to VGND. For the two late transient accuracy samples, evaluate codes 0, 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64, 128 and 255 separately. bit0 is the least significant bit. Each bit starts at 0 V and changes from 10 ns to 10.1 ns to 3.3*bitN V. Simulate through 2 us with a 1 ns requested step. The expected settled voltage is 3.3*code/256 V. Separately, a single static DC sweep maps codes 0 through 255 in 1-code steps to ideal 0/3.3 V bit levels and measures the complete settled transfer. This static sweep has no input transitions and makes no dynamic code-glitch claim. The external drivers and load are testbench components, outside the required passive circuit.

Use reviewed typical primitive models with statistical variation disabled. Numerical parameters from runtime simulation jobs generate the testbench's `parameters.spice`; interpolation and detailed PWL follow the delivered deck. Scope is limited to nominal compact-model conditions, excluding temperature/process corners, mismatch yield, noise, RF/EM and full-chip density closure.

## Electrical Requirements and Scoring

Physical checks and the declared functional bounds must pass. Quality scoring imposes no fixed degradation allowance. Each `source_*` job and its corresponding candidate extracted circuit use exactly the same testbench, model resources, parameters, loads and measurement windows. Independent source observations remain required evidence. The explicit quality targets below supply scoring anchors; otherwise the paired source value is used. Reports retain every paired result.

Let x be the post-layout measurement and b the declared quality target, or the same-condition source measurement when no target is declared:

- Ratio normalization: maximize with q = x/b and minimize with q = b/x. When a numerical scale s is declared, use (x+s)/(b+s) and its reciprocal, respectively. The scale handles zero values as a normalization floor; it is neither an allowed degradation nor an acceptance bound.
- Amplitude in dB: maximize with q = 10^((x-b)/20) and minimize with its reciprocal.
- Target normalization: q = 1/(1+abs(x-b)/s), where s is the declared physical scale. Signed or zero-valued operating points are not divided directly.

Use `layout` scoring. First compute each metric's raw quality q from the worst paired condition, with raw area quality q_area = area_target / candidate_functional_area. Credit c_i = min(1, q_i) for every positively weighted metric, then compute S = 100 * product(c_i ** w_i). The weights sum to 1 and the score is bounded by 100. An improvement beyond one target cannot compensate for missing another target. Physical or functional rejection scores zero; missing or invalid measurements leave the score unknown, and zero-weight observations must also be valid. Reaching every positively weighted target scores 100; a feasible reference may score far below 100. Weights express quality tradeoffs, not acceptance bounds.

For metrics declaring `saturating_ratio`, q = 2(b+s)/(b+x+2*s), using the quality anchor b and the positive scale s in the metric’s units. Equality to the anchor gives raw quality 1; improvements approach raw quality 2 but credited quality stays at 1. Normalize each condition before selecting the worst quality. These scales and targets do not add acceptance cutoffs.

| `accuracy` | Absolute difference between output at 1.9 us and 3.3*code/256, checked independently for each declared code. | V | minimize / saturating_ratio | 0 … +∞ | 5e-06 |

The capability coefficient is **4**, independent of this task's reference-normalized score.

### Quality targets and weights

The area quality target is **4000 um2**, independent of the current feasibility witness. It is not a hard area limit or a process minimum.

The 4000 um2 footprint challenges the physical ladder and routing. Area carries 40%, INL 25%, DNL 20% and late-sample absolute accuracy 15%. The metric named settling is a second late absolute-error sample at 1 us, while accuracy samples at 1.9 us. Both have settled to the same error in current characterization; the earlier sample remains diagnostic with zero weight to avoid counting that equilibrium twice. It is not a measured settling time. Explicit 20 uV and 0.001 LSB goals replace effectively zero ideal-resistor source errors as score anchors, with 5 uV and 0.0005 LSB scales. No dynamic glitch or settling-time capability is claimed.

The targets above affect continuous quality only. A complete feasible reference is allowed to miss them; there is no minimum qualifying score.
