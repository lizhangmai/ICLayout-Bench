# Clocked Capacitive Instrumentation Amplifier with CMFB Layout Task

## Objective

Implement `ia_006_fan_chopper_cmfb`: a complete capacitively coupled differential
instrumentation amplifier with input, feedback and interstage/output chopping,
a two-stage amplifier and transistor common-mode feedback (CMFB). Preserve the
physical signal/feedback capacitors, both bias-return paths, compensation and
the actual common-mode controller. Validate operation with all clocks running.

### Optimization objective

Maximize the declared quality score within the solve budget while satisfying all physical and necessary functional requirements. The first passing DRC, LVS and required electrical checks establish a feasible baseline. Continue improving the scored metrics from that baseline; a passing check or `task_success = true` does not mean that the quality objectives have been achieved.

## Inputs and Interface

- `materials/circuit.cdl`: authoritative physical circuit for native LVS.
- `materials/circuit.spice`: equivalent process-model simulator representation.
- `materials/testbench.spice`: independent bias, clocks, loads and measurements.

The ordered interface is:

```text
vinp vinn voutp voutn vref
clk_chin clk_chin_not clk_chfb clk_chfb_not clk_chout clk_chout_not
vdd vss core__vb1 core__vb2 core__vb3 core__vb4 vref_cm
core__vsum_p core__vsum_n
```

VINP/VINN and VOUTP/VOUTN are the differential input and output. VREF biases
the summing nodes through physical high-poly return arms; VREF_CM is the
reference for the actual transistor common-mode controller. The four CORE__VB
ports supply the declared core bias voltages. The final two ports are
high-impedance summing-node monitors: the testbench observes them without
applying voltage/current drive, a servo or an external load. Keep these named
connections; they also distinguish the symmetric native LVS branches.

Use the fixed models, dimensions, multiplicities and connections in the
netlist. The 46 source MOS groups have a maintained 61-finger implementation;
parallel fingering/merging is allowed when all checks pass. Core matching pairs
have fixed total W*m/L and on-grid finger dimensions; do not replace them with
unqualified model widths. NMOS and resistor bodies connect to SUB through the
explicit VSS substrate tap, and PMOS bodies to WELL through the VDD well tap.
Do not drive the internal CMFB actuator VB4O externally.

Physical passive requirements are:

| Branch | Physical implementation | Nominal value at TT, 27 C |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Each input arm | Four parallel 51.6 × 51.6 um `cap_cmim` units | 16.008384 pF |
| Each capacitive feedback arm | One 23.11 × 23.11 um `cap_cmim` | 0.80480575 pF |
| Each differential Miller path | One 51.6 × 51.6 um `cap_cmim` | 4.002096 pF |
| CMFB actuator to VSS | One 25.85 × 25.85 um `cap_cmim` | 1.00646975 pF |
| Each summing-node return to VREF | 41 series `rhigh` segments, W=1 um, L=180 um, m=1, b=0 | 10.46156 Mohm |
| Each output-to-common-mode-sense arm | Four of the same high-poly segments | 1.02064 Mohm |

The input/feedback capacitor ratio is approximately 19.891. All internal
passives are physical DUT devices, distinct from external output loads. Preserve
MIM plate orientation. The maintained compensation is deliberately retuned for
the physical combined loop; the source's ideal 1 pF Miller / 1 fF controller
values do not define this task.

## Operating Conditions

Use TT MOS/capacitor/resistor models at 27 C and VDD/VSS=1.2/0 V. VREF and
VREF_CM are both 0.6 V. Relative to VSS, CORE__VB1/2/3/4 are respectively
0.5/0.75/0.45/0.589 V. There is no ideal external output-common-mode or
differential-feedback servo. Each output has a 1 pF or 5 pF external load.
At each load, run both signs of a 10 mV differential input step: four conditions.

The input common mode stays at 0.6 V. Both inputs initially equal 0.6 V.
In the differential-response suite, VINP and VINN move oppositely by ±5 mV during 500–501 us, hold for 500 us, then return during 1001–1002 us. A separate common-mode rejection suite holds the differential input at zero and moves both inputs together from 0.6 V to 0.61 V over 1 us at 500 us, holds through 1001 us and returns over 1 us. It runs at both 1 pF and 5 pF loads while all three clock pairs continue switching. The additional `cm_to_dm_peak_v` measurement is the maximum absolute differential output change from its 400–500 us pre-step baseline during 900 us–1 ms.
The pulse period is 10 ms; only the finite 0–1.5 ms sequence is measured.

All three true/complement clock pairs run synchronously at 20 kHz between
0 and 1.2 V. True clocks begin high, fall during 25–25.05 us, hold low for
24.95 us, then rise during 50–50.05 us; the 50 us pattern repeats. Complement
clocks have exactly opposite levels with the same 50 ns edges. Finite
complementary slopes may produce overlap or dead intervals according to device
thresholds; no ideal break-before-make behavior is assumed. Input, feedback and
output/interstage clocks all switch; frozen-clock gain is not an acceptance test.

Transient starts from the solved DC operating point. Use Gear order 2, the
KLU solver, maximum time step 500 ns, `rshunt=1e13`, `reltol=1e-5`,
`abstol=1e-13` and `vntol=1e-8`. Save the declared port voltages and source
currents. The settled observation windows contain integer clock periods;
startup from a zero-supply state is not covered.

## Physical Requirements

Submit GDSII top cell `ia_006_fan_chopper_cmfb`, no larger than 10 MiB.
Pass native IHP main/maximal DRC without waivers (standalone scope with
density/antenna disabled), strict named-interface LVS including taps, and a
3000 × 700 um functional outline. Device, contact and complete routing layers
are enumerated by the runtime outline constraint. Pin/annotation layers are
excluded from area and cannot conceal functional geometry.

The submitted layout must provide the actual capacitors, resistor segments and
matching-aware core. Post-layout simulation consumes candidate-derived Magic
RC with device junction geometry and interconnect. Extraction subdivides the
initial Magic grid by two and uses `gds_readonly=false` on an isolated import;
the submitted GDS remains immutable. No extraction diagnostic is waived.
Magic idealizes the explicit tap devices while retaining its extracted RC
network; the source simulator includes finite tap models. Distributed substrate
noise and manufacturing signoff are not qualified.

## Electrical Requirements and Scoring

Artifact, geometry, DRC, LVS and declared functional requirements must pass. Parasitic extraction is also mandatory: it must produce a complete, electrically consistent netlist usable by the required post-layout simulations. A completed candidate rejection scores zero; infrastructure errors do not establish candidate failure.

For `layout` scoring, cap every positively weighted raw quality at 1 before aggregation; the score ranges from 0 to 100. Reaching every weighted target scores 100, and no minimum score is required for validity.

Physical checks and the declared functional bounds must pass. Each `source_*` job and its corresponding candidate extracted circuit use exactly the same testbench, model resources, parameters, loads and measurement windows. Every electrical scoring anchor is the paired pre-layout measurement under that condition. The score measures preservation of the fixed circuit's performance; it does not require layout to improve intrinsic circuit behavior. Reports retain every paired result.

Let x be the post-layout measurement and b the same-condition pre-layout measurement:

- Ratio normalization: maximize with q = x/b and minimize with q = b/x. When a numerical scale s is declared, use (x+s)/(b+s) and its reciprocal, respectively. The scale handles zero values as a normalization floor; it is neither an allowed degradation nor an acceptance bound.
- Amplitude in dB: maximize with q = 10^((x-b)/20) and minimize with its reciprocal.
- Target normalization: q = 1/(1+abs(x-b)/s), where s is the declared physical scale. Signed or zero-valued operating points are not divided directly.

Use `layout` scoring. First compute each metric's raw quality q from the worst paired condition, with raw area quality q_area = area_target / candidate_functional_area. Credit c_i = min(1, q_i) for every positively weighted metric, then compute S = 100 * product(c_i ** w_i). The weights sum to 1 and the score is bounded by 100. An improvement beyond one target cannot compensate for missing another target. Physical, extraction, or functional rejection scores zero; other missing or invalid measurements leave the score unknown, and zero-weight observations must also be valid. Reaching every positively weighted target scores 100; a feasible reference may score far below 100. Weights express quality tradeoffs, not acceptance bounds.

The measurements below use the supplied SPICE node and source names. `v(n)` is a node voltage; `i(V)` is current flowing into a voltage source, so delivered power uses a negative sign. `db(z)=20*log10(abs(z))`; `mag/abs` denote magnitude, and `cph` denotes continuous phase in radians. `find`, `when`, `from/to` and `rise/fall` follow the deck's interpolation, window and crossing conventions. SPICE suffixes p/n/u/m/meg/g mean pico/nano/micro/milli/mega/giga. Nested `avg`, `min`, `max`, `vecmin/vecmax` and `deriv` represent window averages, extrema and sampled derivatives. Measure each operating condition independently, pair it with the source result under that condition, and take the worst paired quality within each metric group.

For metrics declaring `saturating_ratio`, q = 2(b+s)/(b+x+2*s), using the quality anchor b and the positive scale s in the metric’s units. Equality to the anchor gives raw quality 1; improvements approach raw quality 2 but credited quality stays at 1. Normalize each condition before selecting the worst quality. These scales and targets do not add acceptance cutoffs.

### Quality targets and weights

The area quality target is **34000 um2**, with **20%** weight. It is an integration objective, not a hard limit or a proven achievable minimum.

The 13 fixed MIM plates occupy 28361.9667 um2, or 83.4% of the 34000 um2 footprint objective, leaving 5638.0333 um2 of planar overhead. High-poly chains, taps, MOS, plate spacing and routing must be integrated using the permitted layer stack; their layer areas are not added to MIM area where legal overlap is possible. This is a demanding footprint objective, not a proven achievable minimum or an acceptance bound. Clock power carries 30%, baseline error 25%, area 20%, return error 10%, peak-to-peak ripple 10% and gain preservation 5%. These source-paired metrics emphasize clock-routing cost and offset recovery while all clocks run. RMS ripple, common-mode conversion and mean rail power remain diagnostics. The existing error floors, complete condition set and functional requirements are retained.

Peak-to-peak ripple has 10% weight and retains the complete 900 us-1 ms window, so clock spikes remain visible. RMS ripple is diagnostic. Baseline and return errors retain their 0.1 uV normalization floor, equal to 10 ppm of the 10 mV stimulus. Clock power retains the source-paired positive-energy accounting while every clock pair switches.

## Tools and Submission

Soft solve budget: **1.5 hours**.

Discover frozen task inputs, requirements, reviewed resources and harness
feedback in `/protocol/task.json`, the `resources` field of `/protocol/task.json` and
`/protocol/harness.json`. Use the supplied IHP primitives and KLayout, Magic
and ngspice. Write `/workspace/output/final.gds` and explicitly submit through
the harness protocol. Source records, reference GDS and host configuration
remain outside standard solver inputs.

### Solve workflow

1. Read the declared circuit and materials, `/protocol/task.json` for paths, constraints and reviewed resources, and `/protocol/harness.json` for capabilities and helper commands. Task inputs under `/task` and resources under `/resources` are read-only; construct the layout in `/workspace` with the declared tools. With layout MCP, paths and commands refer to the remote EDA workspace. Create your own implementation with real devices, interconnect and pins, using your own tool loop without delegating to other agents; reference layouts and author code are not solver inputs.
2. Inspect `resources.external` before using external tools. Each command starts a fresh shell: when tool modules are declared, source `/usr/share/Modules/init/bash` and load the selected tool's modules; export its declared `tool_environment` settings in that same command. Repeat as needed and use batch or headless operation. Local manuals, command references, and release notes may be available within the declared EDA installation and PDK resource directories. Locate these resources through `/protocol/task.json` and consult the documentation for the installed tool version when diagnosing unfamiliar syntax or tool failures.
3. Generate an initial candidate and submit a snapshot early. When `process-feedback` is available, check the current GDS with layout MCP `check` or `python -I /protocol/process_check.py` before the final submission. This runs the final evaluator's complete plan and returns engineering diagnostics and candidate measurements, without scores or normalized quality factors. Checks have no count limit and consume the same wall-clock budget. Inspect the physical and functional results and all scored metrics; successful tool execution alone does not establish requirement satisfaction. Read paginated diagnostics with `report` when using MCP. Local exploratory checks do not replace this complete check.
4. Use the remaining time for measured improvements to the declared quality objectives. Identify the limiting scored metrics, make targeted layout changes, regenerate and check the candidate, and compare measurements with the previous candidate and the stated scoring targets and trade-offs. Preserve the best valid candidate and revert changes that worsen the overall objective. Repeat while time and useful improvements remain; passing validity checks alone is not a stopping condition.
5. Stop optimization when the budget expires, all declared quality objectives are achieved, or measured trials support convergence. If stopping early, explain the changes tried, the metric comparisons and the remaining limitations. Track remaining time through session status. At soft-budget expiry, finish or cancel already-started work and submit immediately; do not start another command, check or optimization round. If an MCP execution is running, use `poll` with its `execution_id` and the returned `next_offset` as `offset` until completion, or cancel it and wait for it to stop.
6. Finish or cancel outstanding work, restore your best candidate to the declared output path, and explicitly submit it before ending the session. With layout MCP `submit`, remove the `/workspace/` prefix from `output.path`; with the container interface, run `python -I /protocol/submit.py`. Wait for the receipt: only the last accepted snapshot is judged, and writing a file alone is not submission. Acceptance confirms delivery; the framework independently evaluates validity and quality after the session.
