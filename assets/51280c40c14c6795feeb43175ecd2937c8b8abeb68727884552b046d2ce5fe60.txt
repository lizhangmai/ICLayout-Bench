# Ramos PFC Three-Stage Amplifier Layout Task

## Objective

Implement `amp_014_ramos_pfc` with the supplied fixed topology and dimensions. The PMOS input pair drives the cascoded NMOS branches at dm_2/net063; the voutn mirror produces net050. XM10 and the XM21/XM22 mirror drive net049, while XM11/XM23 drive the single-ended output. Both compensation paths remain: 63 pF from net050 to vout and 25 pF from net050 to net049. The vb3/vb4 bias network is retained.
Do not replace the circuit, delete compensation, or add a servo. The circuit contains 24 logical MOS groups with the upstream W, L, multiplicity
and four-terminal connections. PMOS groups XM8 and XM9 both have source and body
on net31; other PMOS bodies connect to vdd, and NMOS bodies connect to vss.

## Inputs and Interface

`materials/circuit.cdl` is the authoritative logical circuit with native PDK model bindings, `materials/circuit.spice` its simulation representation, `materials/testbench.spice` defines closed-loop observations, and `materials/ac.spice` defines balanced differential AC excitation. Ordered ports are `vss vdd vinn vinp vout net1`: ground, supply, negative/positive inputs, single-ended output and an external bias-current pull-down. Bias, input drive, feedback connections and loads are external test fixtures. Implement the compensation capacitors and physical body contacts. Body contacts are layout connectivity and are not additional logical circuit devices.

## Operating Conditions

IHP SG13G2, nominal TT/typical, 27 C, supply 1.5 V, input reference 0.4 V,
external current sink 2.4e-05 A to vss, and 10 pF output load. Use the fixed IHP LV binding at 1.5 V, adapting the authored 1.8 V stimulus to the LV process rail; retain its bias current and input reference. Round W/L to 5 nm (the supplied integer-micrometre sizes are unchanged).
The closed-loop deck ties vinn directly to vout, drives vinp with a +100 mV pulse
starting at 20 us (100 ns rise/fall, 80 us high duration), and runs to 200 us with
10 ns maximum steps, Gear order 2, reltol=1e-5, abstol=1 pA, vntol=10 nV.
Transient starts from the solved DC point; this is not a zero-state supply-ramp
startup test. High and return windows are 80–100 and 180–200 us.

AC sweeps 200 points per decade from 1 Hz to 1 GHz. The differential-mode deck retains output DC feedback through a 1 TH inductor and isolates the bias node from AC through a 1 F capacitor to ground; vinp/vinn use balanced +0.5/-0.5 V small-signal excitation. Input common-mode residual amplitude and DC feedback error must both be <=1 uV. These are external small-signal bias fixtures, not physical compensation; an independent closed-loop deck measures actual follower response and does not call a voltage-transfer estimate a loop return ratio.

## Electrical Requirements and Scoring

Physical checks and the declared functional bounds must pass. Quality scoring imposes no fixed degradation allowance. Each `source_*` job and its corresponding candidate extracted circuit use exactly the same testbench, model resources, parameters, loads and measurement windows. Independent source observations remain required evidence. The explicit quality targets below supply scoring anchors; otherwise the paired source value is used. Reports retain every paired result.

Let x be the post-layout measurement and b the declared quality target, or the same-condition source measurement when no target is declared:

- Ratio normalization: maximize with q = x/b and minimize with q = b/x. When a numerical scale s is declared, use (x+s)/(b+s) and its reciprocal, respectively. The scale handles zero values as a normalization floor; it is neither an allowed degradation nor an acceptance bound.
- Amplitude in dB: maximize with q = 10^((x-b)/20) and minimize with its reciprocal.
- Target normalization: q = 1/(1+abs(x-b)/s), where s is the declared physical scale. Signed or zero-valued operating points are not divided directly.

Use `layout` scoring. First compute each metric's raw quality q from the worst paired condition, with raw area quality q_area = area_target / candidate_functional_area. Credit c_i = min(1, q_i) for every positively weighted metric, then compute S = 100 * product(c_i ** w_i). The weights sum to 1 and the score is bounded by 100. An improvement beyond one target cannot compensate for missing another target. Physical or functional rejection scores zero; missing or invalid measurements leave the score unknown, and zero-weight observations must also be valid. Reaching every positively weighted target scores 100; a feasible reference may score far below 100. Weights express quality tradeoffs, not acceptance bounds.

`late_error_v`, `return_error_v`, `ripple_v` and `return_ripple_v` use `saturating_ratio`. Candidate error x, declared target (or paired source) b and the positive scale s below define q = 2(b+s)/(b+x+2s). Equality to the anchor gives raw q=1; decreasing error approaches raw quality 2, while credited quality stays at 1. The scale retains its declared unit and numerical-floor meaning. Normalize each condition before taking the worst paired quality; other metrics retain their declared normalization and functional requirements.

The common-mode test-apparatus bound validates differential excitation, rather than DUT common-mode regulation. The DC feedback bound excludes failure of the isolated bias solve. Both apparatus checks apply to source and candidate simulation before scoring. These are this task's hard electrical requirements; upstream paper targets are not acceptance bounds. Window error and ripple do not establish settling or periodic steady state; no phase margin, unity-gain crossing, settling time, CMRR, noise, PVT or mismatch performance is claimed.

Error metrics use saturating normalization `2*(b+s)/(b+x+2*s)`, with the quality anchor and scale declared below. This differs from an unbounded inverse ratio. Zero-weight ripple, return error, bias and closed-loop gain remain diagnostics; the response integral separately scores dynamic quality. Capability coefficient **7** is independent of the score.

### Quality targets and weights

The area quality target is **70000 um2**. It is an integration budget, not a hard limit or a proven process minimum.

The 23 fixed MIM plates occupy 58539.3916 um2. A 70000 um2 integration budget allocates about 84% to those plates, leaving 11460.6084 um2 for enclosures, MOS devices, body contacts and interconnect; it is not a minimum-area theorem. Area carries 35%, 100 ppm step-referred late accuracy 15%, the worst bilateral normalized response integral 20%, 10 kHz gain 15%, DC gain 10% and mean power 5%. The response integral removes each final plateau offset, so dynamic response and static accuracy are distinct. Repeated return offset, ripple and operating-point summaries remain diagnostic.

`response_time_s` takes the worse normalized absolute-error integral: integrate `abs(v(vout)-high_v)/0.1` over 20.1–100 us, or `abs(v(vout)-low_v)/0.1` over 100.2–180 us. The existing high/return means supply the final plateau values. The 75 ns response-integral budget is a layout-delay quality target under the declared 100 mV step and 10 pF load; it is not an acceptance threshold. Removing each plateau offset isolates dynamic response from the separately scored static error. This finite-window integral is in seconds; it is not a threshold-crossing settling time and does not establish periodic steady state.
